          National Health Authority (NHA) plays a pivotal role in strengthening India’s health
          financing and service delivery ecosystem through the implementation and stewardship
          of key national health initiatives, most notably Ayushman Bharat Pradhan Mantri Jan
          Arogya Yojana (AB PM-JAY) and the Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission (ABDM). Over the
          years, as an attached office of the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare, NHA has issued
          a wide range of operational guidelines, policy advisories, standard operating procedures,
          and implementation frameworks to support States, Union Territories, insurers, hospitals,
          and other stakeholders in ensuring the effective and uniform implementation of these
          programmes across the country.

          Given the scale and complexity of India’s health system, with diverse administrative
          capacities, institutional arrangements, and service delivery contexts across States and
          Union Territories, it has become increasingly important to consolidate these directives
          into a single, accessible reference resource.
PREFACE




          This Compendium of Guidelines for AB PM-JAY has therefore been developed as a
          comprehensive repository of key guidelines issued by NHA, bringing together critical
          policy documents and operational instructions related to implementation of PM-
          JAY in one place. The compendium aims to enhance clarity, promote consistency in
          implementation, and facilitate easier access to authoritative guidance for all stakeholders
          involved in the execution of the scheme.

          The compendium will serve multiple purposes. First, it will act as a central reference
          document for State Health Agencies, implementation support agencies, insurers, third-
          party administrators, empanelled hospitals, and partner organizations for PM-JAY. By
          consolidating guidelines that were previously issued across different time periods and
          formats, it will help users navigate the regulatory and operational framework more
          efficiently. Second, it contributes to institutional memory and knowledge management
          within the health system by systematically documenting the evolution of policies,
          processes, and operational standards developed by NHA. Third, it supports capacity
          building and training efforts, enabling stakeholders at national, state, and district levels
          to better understand the programmatic requirements and implementation protocols.

          The guidelines included in this compendium span a wide range of thematic areas

          redressal mechanisms, IEC, finance and capacity-building initiatives. Together, these
          guidelines provide a structured framework that ensures transparency, accountability,

                 Guidelines for Pradhan Mantri Arogya
                   Mitra (PMAMs) under AB PM-JAY
Ayushman Bharat – Pradhan Mantri Jan Arogya Yojana (AB PM-JAY) is a flagship Government of India initiative
that provides health assurance coverage of up to ₹5 lakh per family per year for secondary and tertiary care
hospitalisation to over 15.14 crore eligible beneficiary families (PM-JAY, VVS and ASHA, Anganwadi workers
and helpers). The scheme offers free, cashless and paperless treatment at empanelled public and private
hospitals, with benefits that are portable across the country.
The implementation of AB PM-JAY entails multiple complex and technology-driven functions, including
beneficiary enrolment and verification, hospital empanelment, pre-authorization and claims management, IT
platform operations, and grievance redressal at the State/UT level.
To streamline the health service delivery and provide a seamless experience to the beneficiary, a ‘Pradhan
Mantri Arogya Mitra’ (PMAM) is deployed at each empanelled hospital to facilitate beneficiaries in accessing
healthcare services under AB PM-JAY.
This guideline under Ayushman Bharat–Pradhan Mantri Jan Arogya Yojana (AB PM-JAY) provide a structured
framework for engaging Pradhan Mantri Arogya Mitras (PMAMs) to support States/UTs and empanelled
healthcare providers (EHCPs) in strengthening implementation across the key functional areas of the scheme.

1. PMAM Governance Framework				
1.1 Who is a Pradhan Mantri Arogya Mitra (PMAM)?
    A PMAM is a verified frontline health service professional who shall be present at each of the Empanelled
    Health Care Provider (EHCP) and shall serve as a first point of contact for the beneficiaries. The Pradhan
    Mantri Arogya Mitra (PMAM) serves as a bridge between beneficiaries, empanelled hospitals, state
    health agencies and the AB PM-JAY system, ensuring that eligible patients are guided, well informed, and
    supported throughout the process of availing treatment under the assurance scheme.

1.2 Deployment of PMAM in EHCPs
    PMAMs shall be placed at every public and private EHCP. The number of PMAMs to be deployed may be
    determined by taking the following points into account.
    I.    The number of PMAMs to be deputed in an EHCP shall be determined based on the patient load.
    II.   Deployment of PMAMs shall be planned in accordance with operational requirements and shift
          schedules.
    III. A minimum of two PMAMs shall be deployed per shift. However, the State may exercise flexibility to
         modify the deployment based on specific requirements and available facilities.
          *State/EHCP shall ensure that services are available to beneficiaries round the clock (24×7) in the
          hospital, particularly at emergency counters. After the initial placement, the SHA may consider
          rotating PMAMs every 8-12 months within the same city or town. *

1.3 Selection Criteria and Compliance Requirements
    Pradhan Mantri Arogya Mitras (PMAMs) shall be selected based on the following criteria:
    I.    The minimum educational qualification shall be 10+2 from a recognised institution (preferably
          graduation)
    II.   Candidates with experience of 3 yrs or more with a healthcare background shall be given preference.


                                                     13
   III. The candidate should be fluent in the local language of the State/region and shall have a working
        knowledge of English and Hindi.
   IV. Preference shall be given to candidates possessing adequate functional computer literacy, including
       proficiency in the Microsoft Office Suite and the ability to navigate internet-based portals.
   The following compliance requirements shall be followed by SHAs and EHCPs during PMAM selection:
   I.      The SHA and EHCPs shall ensure that candidates deployed as PMAMs are competent, adequately
           trained, possess strong communication skills with proficiency in the local language and consistently
           demonstrate a helpful and courteous approach while interacting with patients and their family
           members.
   II.     The SHA / EHCPs / third-party agency / BFA shall conduct due diligence to verify the character and
           antecedents of candidates prior to their deployment as PMAMs, ensuring that they have no adverse
           police records or pending criminal cases.
   III. The SHA /EHCPs / third-party agency / BFA shall collect and verify proof of identity, including a
        recent photograph and Aadhaar card, prior to deployment.
   IV. The SHA / EHCPs / third-party agency / BFA shall replace the PMAM deployed at the concerned
       EHCP in cases of misconduct, breach of integrity, and/or abuse of position.

1.5 Remuneration and Benefits for PMAMs
   The monthly remuneration of PMAMs, including fixed components and additional incentives, shall not be
   less than the minimum wages prescribed under the Minimum Wages Act.

   1.5.1 For Public EHCPs
   For all the PMAMs working in public facilities, benefits shall be paid by the SHA / BFA/ EHCP. The suggested
   benefits for the PMAM can be done in two ways:
   I.    A fixed honorarium with case-based incentive OR
   II.   A fixed remuneration per month
         Honorarium / salary of PMAM may be paid from the Rogi Kalyan Samiti of EHCPs or any other
         source.

   1.5.2 For Private EHCPs
   I.    For all PMAMs employed in Private EHCPs, benefits shall be provided by the Private EHCP.
   II.   The nature and structure of benefits shall be determined by the Private EHCP in accordance with the
         Minimum Wages Act.
   III. The monthly remuneration of PMAMs may include fixed components and additional incentives, if
        applicable.
   Incentives may be linked to the KPIs defined for PMAMs in Annexure 1; however, the proposed incentive
   structure is indicative in nature. States with a different contractual structure may design and implement
   their own incentive mechanisms for PMAMs.

1.6 Terms of engagement of PMAM
   A PMAM may be engaged initially for a period of one year, with the contract subject to annual renewal
   based on performance against the key performance indicators defined by the State Health Agency (SHA).

2.1 Registration on User Management Portal (UMP)
   Registration on the User Management Portal (UMP) through Aadhaar-based authentication is mandatory
   for all deployed PMAMs to access any application within the AB PM-JAY IT ecosystem.
   Under the User Management Portal (UMP), PMAM user roles are mapped to the respective Empanelled
   Health Care Provider (EHCP) and approved through the EHCP admin login.

   He/she shall be responsible for the Transaction Management System (TMS) for pre-authorisation, claim
   processing, and related activities under the guidance of MEDCO, in accordance with the guidelines issued
   by the SHA/NHA, as amended from time to time.

3. Roles and Responsibilities					
3.1 Roles and Responsibilities of PMAM
   Under AB PM-JAY, an PMAM performs three key roles: first, as a trusted partner of beneficiaries; second,
   as a representative of the EHCPs; and third, as a system operator. The PMAM serves as the primary point
   of contact for beneficiaries at each EHCP and is responsible for the following broad functional areas:
   I.    Guiding beneficiaries on the overall benefits available under PM-JAY and facilitating timely access
         to the eligible beneficiaries for availing treatment at empanelled healthcare providers (EHCPs). The
         PMAM shall be responsible for verifying and communicating to citizens whether they are eligible to
         avail benefits under the PM-JAY scheme.
   II.   The PMAM shall clearly communicate to beneficiaries that OPD services are not covered under PM-
         JAY.
   III. The PMAM shall be responsible for operating the Beneficiary Identification System (BIS) for the
        identification and verification of PM-JAY–eligible beneficiaries.
   IV.   The PMAM shall have access to the Transaction Management System (TMS) to manage hospital-
         level transaction functions. This includes scanning and uploading all documents required for pre-
         authorization requests, updating surgery and treatment details, filing claims, and verifying discharge
         summaries in coordination with the medical coordinator of the EHCP.
   V.    The PMAM is also responsible for responding to queries or providing additional information to the
         Insurance Company, State Health Agency (SHA), ISA, or other relevant authorities, ensuring timely
         and accurate communication for smooth claim and treatment processing.
   VI. The PMAM shall be responsible for operating the Hospital Empanelment Module (HEM) by completing
       all required details in consultation with and approval from the concerned EHCP. In addition, the
       PMAM shall regularly update EHCP details in the Health Facility Registry (HFR).
   VII. The PMAM shall be responsible for ensuring that the patient, from the time of pre-authorization to
        discharge, is getting all the benefits as per AB PM-JAY norms.
   VIII. The PMAM shall be responsible for liaising with the EHCP to facilitate timely patient admission and
         ensure the availability of beds. The PMAM shall liaise and coordinate with the Medical Officer /
         MEDCO to collect, scan, and upload all requisite documents for submission of claim requests.
   IX. In the event of loss or damage to an Ayushman Card, the PMAM shall guide and assist the cardholder
       in obtaining a duplicate copy of the Ayushman Card.

3.2 Responsibilities of Empanelled Hospitals regarding PMAMs under AB PM-JAY
   EHCPs shall play a crucial role in enabling PMAMs to function effectively as the last-mile facilitators
   of AB PM-JAY. Adequate institutional support, effective coordination, and strict adherence to scheme
   guidelines by EHCPs are critical to improving beneficiary experience, enhancing utilisation, and ensuring
   transparency and accountability under the scheme.
   I.     EHCPs shall provide adequate and dedicated workspace to PMAMs, including a helpdesk or
          facilitation counter at prominent locations. The EHCP shall ensure availability of basic infrastructure
          such as seating, power supply, internet connectivity, and access to computers / printers required.

                                                       18
   The indicative list of equipment to be installed by each EHCP for the effective functioning of PMAMs
   includes the following (but is not limited to):
           a. Computer/ laptop with the latest browser
           b. Internet connectivity (a preferred bandwidth of 2 Mbps or above)
           c. QR Code Scanner
           d. Document scanner to scan requisite documents
           e. Printer
           f.   Web camera for photo
           g. Device for fingerprint, face auth and IRIS biometrics
           h. Stationary Items
   II.   EHCPs shall involve PMAMs into patient admission, discharge, and billing workflows related to AB-
         PMJAY. Treating departments, admission desks, and billing sections shall coordinate with PMAMs
         to ensure timely initiation of pre-authorisation, smooth beneficiary admission and compliance with
         scheme guidelines.
   III. EHCPs shall provide PMAMs access to ward to ensure coverage of any left-out patients in the wards.
        If, at any point of time, a patient possessing a valid Ayushman card is present in the ward, it shall
        be the responsibility of the PMAM to facilitate admission of such patients under the scheme, in
        accordance with the applicable AB PM-JAY guidelines.
   IV. EHCPs shall provide PMAMs access to relevant patient records, clinical documents, and administrative
       information required for pre-authorisation, claims processing, audits, and grievance redressal, in
       accordance with data privacy and confidentiality norms.
   V.    EHCPs shall adhere to AB PM-JAY guidelines, package rates, and Standard Operating Procedures
         (SOPs) and shall cooperate with PMAMs in ensuring compliance at all stages of treatment. Any
         deviation observed by PMAMs shall be promptly addressed by EHCP.
   VI. EHCPS shall cooperate with PMAMs in resolving beneficiary grievances at the facility level. EHCP
       authorities shall designate a nodal officer to coordinate with PMAMs, SHAs, and NHA for timely
       resolution of complaints and corrective action.
   VII. EHCPs shall allow PMAMs to perform their duties independently and professionally, without any
        pressure or interference. PMAMs should not be assigned tasks unrelated to AB PM-JAY or used in
        place of hospital administrative staff
   VIII. EHCPs shall facilitate PMAM participation in training, reviews, audits, and capacity-building activities
         organised by SHAs / NHA. Hospital management shall ensure timely dissemination of scheme
         updates, circulars, and SOP revisions to PMAMs.
   IX. EHCPs shall coordinate with PMAMs during audits, inspections, and verification exercises by SHAs,
       NHA, or third-party agencies, including timely submission of records and compliance with audit
       observations.
   X.    EHCPs shall be accountable for actions taken at the facility level under AB PM-JAY and shall cooperate
         with PMAMs in reporting utilisation data, grievances, and operational issues accurately and in a
         timely manner.

   I.    SHA shall ensure that the deployed PMAMs adhere to the operational guidelines published by NHA /
         SHA / EHCPs, as amended from time to time. In the event of any irregularities, the SHA shall initiate
         disciplinary proceedings against errant PMAMs and/or the deployed BFA(s), in accordance with
         applicable contractual provisions and guidelines.

    II.   SHA shall develop a mechanism to track the productivity of individual PMAMs
    III. SHA shall ensure that deployed PMAMs are paid their salaries and any other perks / incentives in a

    IV. The SHA shall establish a patient feedback mechanism and incorporate the same into the appraisal
        of PMAMs. Such information shall also be shared with the NHA.
    V.    SHAs shall be responsible to close all BFA agency logins during agency replacement and create new

    VI. SHA shall ensure that deployed PMAMs attend all capacity-building sessions organised by the NHA
        or SHA. Further, PMAMs shall complete the certification process as prescribed by the NHA/SHA,

    VII. Ensure quarterly performance review of deployed PMAM are conducted by BFA / EHCPs in joint

    VIII. Issue necessary instructions to public EHCPs to provide requisite support to the empanelled agency
          in fulfilling its contractual obligations.
    IX. SHA shall issue necessary (State specific) guidelines for the patients in EHCP to cooperate with the
        PMAM and provide him/her necessary information/document to verify the eligibility under the scheme.


4. Capacity Building of PMAMs						
Capacity building for Pradhan Mantri Arogya Mitras (PMAMs) shall be undertaken through a structured
framework aimed at ensuring that each PMAM is comprehensively trained in their roles and responsibilities.
The framework seeks to institutionalize a continuous, technology-enabled capacity-building mechanism
through a Learning Management System (LMS) to enable consistent knowledge dissemination, skill
enhancement, adherence to Ayushman Bharat–Pradhan Mantri Jan Arogya Yojana (AB PM-JAY) guidelines,
and overall improvement in scheme utilization and beneficiary experience.

4.1 Compliance with LMS-enabled capacity building
    I.    Registration on LMS: All PMAMs engaged under AB-PMJAY shall be mandatorily registered on the
          designated Learning Management System (LMS) prescribed by the National Health Authority (NHA).
    II.   Regular participation: Regular login and participation on the LMS shall be compulsory for all
          PMAMs for accessing training content, completing assessments, and maintaining certification status.
    III. Quarterly Capacity Building through LMS: Quarterly capacity-building programmes shall
         be delivered through a blended learning approach, with core modules hosted on the LMS and
         supplemented by classroom-based or virtual sessions, as required.
    IV. Activity status on LMS: LMS login credentials shall be treated as official credentials, and non-usage
        or prolonged inactivity on the LMS shall be considered as non-compliance with capacity-building
        requirements. Each PMAM shall be required to:
            a. Log in to the LMS at least once during each quarter
            b. Complete prescribed quarterly learning modules
            c. Appear for online assessments hosted on the LMS
    V.    LMS as the Single Source of Truth: The LMS shall function as the single source of truth for tracking
          PMAM training attendance, module completion, assessment scores, and certification status.



                                                     20
   VI. Monitoring by SHA: SHAs shall monitor LMS dashboards on a periodic basis and submit consolidated
       compliance reports to NHA.
   VII. Consequences of LMS Non-Compliance: Repeated non-compliance with LMS requirements may
        result in:
         a. Mandatory refresher training
         b. Conditional suspension of benefits
         c. Disengagement of PMAMs, as per prescribed norms

4.2 Topics Covered under the LMS (Quarterly)
   a.    Phase I: AB-PM-JAY and ABDM Scheme overview, beneficiary eligibility, entitlements, Ayushman
         card usage, facilitation and communication skills
   b.    Phase II: Admission workflow, pre-authorisation, documentation standards, package selection, and
         claims processes
   c.    Phase III: BIS, TMS, claims portals, data quality, cybersecurity, and dashboard usage
   d.    Phase IV: SOP compliance, quality assurance, grievance redressal, audit readiness, and ethical
         conduct

4.3 Refresher and Need-Based Trainings via LMS
   I.    Refresher training arising from policy changes, IT system upgrades, or identified performance gaps
         shall be mandatorily delivered and tracked through the LMS.
   II.   Completion of refresher modules within prescribed timelines shall be compulsory for:
         a. Newly onboarded PMAMs
         b. PMAMs identified as low performing
         c. PMAMs shall adapt to major updates in guidelines, policies, or IT systems that affect their roles,
            responsibilities, or system access.

4.4 Assessment, Certification, and Re-certification Framework
   I.    All assessments shall be conducted exclusively through the LMS to ensure standardisation,
         transparency, and auditability.
   II.   PMAMs shall be required to secure the minimum qualifying score, as prescribed by NHA/SHAs, to
         obtain or retain certification.
   III. Certification and re-certification status shall be auto generated and recorded on the LMS and shall be
        a prerequisite for:
         a. Continuation of engagement or deployment at EHCPs
         b. Eligibility for performance-based incentives
         c. Incentives may be withheld, reduced, or deferred in cases of:
            •   Non-registration on LMS
            •   Failure to qualify prescribed assessments

4.5 Institutional Roles and Responsibilities
   I.    NHA shall:
         a. Designate and maintain the national LMS platform


                                                     21
           b. Upload standardised training content and assessments
           c. Define certification norms and LMS compliance benchmarks
   II.      State Health Agencies (SHAs) shall:
           a. Ensure LMS onboarding of all PMAMs
           b. Monitor login frequency, completion rates, and assessment outcomes
           c. Take corrective actions in cases of non-compliance
   III. Empanelled Hospitals shall:
           a. Facilitate PMAM access to computers/internet for LMS usage
           b. Ensure PMAMs are provided time and institutional support to complete LMS modules

4.6 In-person Training
   In addition to the learning modules on the LMS, offline capacity-building sessions will be scheduled in
   advance. PMAMs shall be duly informed and are required to attend these sessions.


5. Do’s / Don’ts for PMAM							
5.1 Do’s
     I.       The PMAM shall greet beneficiaries courteously at the beginning of every interaction and maintain
              a polite, respectful, and empathetic demeanour throughout the engagement
     II.      The PMAM shall ensure the privacy and confidentiality of all beneficiary documents and personal
              information, including but not limited to Aadhaar cards, ration cards, prescriptions, treatment
              details, and mobile numbers.
     III.     The PMAM shall inform the beneficiary of all patient rights at the time of discharge.
     IV.      The PMAM shall ensure that the beneficiary receives the prescribed medicines.
     V.       The PMAM shall ensure that grievances are promptly raised and escalated to the concerned
              authorities for resolution.
     VI.      The PMAM shall provide complete and accurate patient-related information to the patient party.
     VII. The PMAM shall ensure that adequate connectivity and necessary equipment are available for
          smooth operations.
     VIII. The PMAM shall be available at the help desk at all times during assigned duty hours.
     IX.      The PMAM shall be neatly and professionally dressed in the approved uniform during working
              hours.

5.2 Don’ts
     I.       The PMAM shall always uphold ethical conduct and shall not engage in any activity involving
              unethical demands or practices.
     II.      The PMAM shall not engage in any form of misbehaviour while interacting with beneficiaries,
              hospital staff, or other stakeholders.
     III.     The PMAM shall not leave the designated desk unattended during working hours.

PMAMs are central to the effective delivery of services under the PM-JAY scheme and are required to function in

from SHAs and EHCPs. Ongoing capacity building through the LMS, along with the effective use of digital
platforms such as BIS, TMS, and the Ayushman App, will further enhance implementation outcomes. All

Guidelines for Financial Management under AB PM-JAY
Ayushman Bharat Pradhan Mantri – Jan Arogya Yojana (AB PM-JAY) is the world’s largest health assurance
scheme that intends to reduce the out-of-pocket expenditure arising due to catastrophic expenditure on
healthcare. AB PM-JAY was launched on 23rd September 2018 as recommended by the National Health Policy
2017, to achieve the SDGs vision of Universal Health Coverage (UHC), subsuming the erstwhile Rashtriya
Swasthya Bima Yojana (RSBY). The scheme provides health cover of Rs. 5 lakhs per family per year for secondary
and tertiary care hospitalization to bottom 40% of the India’s poor and vulnerable population.

1. Background and Purpose			
1.1 Introduction
      1.1.1   Initially, 10.74 crore eligible households were identified under the scheme from SECC 2011 based
              on certain deprivation and occupational criteria in rural and urban areas respectively. Further, in
              January 2022, the Cabinet to the Government of India revised the beneficiary based to 12 crore
              families, considering the decadal population growth rate of 11.7% and gave the flexibility to
              States/UTs to use other State scheme databases.
      1.1.2   In March 2024, 37 lakh families of Accredited Social Health Activists (ASHAs), Anganwadi Workers
              (AWWs) and Anganwadi Helpers (AWHs) were also included in the scheme.
      1.1.3   Further, on 29th October 2024, the Government has expanded AB PM-JAY to provide free treatment
              benefits of up to ₹5 lakh per year to all senior citizens aged 70 years and above along with their
              families, irrespective of their socio-economic status.
      1.1.4   This consolidated guideline is issued to bring all operative financial provisions under a single
              reference document - covering fund release for implementation and administrative purposes
              across all modes of implementation and for all eligible beneficiary families covered under AB PM-
              JAY categories.
1.2 Scope and Applicability
      1.2.1   These guidelines shall apply to all States and Union Territories implementing AB PM-JAY, their
              respective State Health Agencies (SHAs), District Implementation Units (DIUs), and other
              implementation partners (i.e. banks, insurance companies, etc) involved in the financial operations
              of the scheme.
      1.2.2   The provisions herein govern:
              a.   Release of GIA for implementation and administrative purposes across all modes of
                   implementation - Insurance Mode, Trust Mode, and Mixed Mode - in respect of AB PM-JAY
                   beneficiaries, ASHA/AWW/AWH beneficiaries and Ayushman Bharat Vay Vandana Scheme
                   beneficiaries.
              b.   Banking and escrow account operations.
              c.   Utilization certification and audit compliance.
1.3   Beneficiary Coverage: These guidelines apply in respect of the AB PM-JAY beneficiaries, ASHA/AWW/
      AWH beneficiaries, and Ayushman Bharat Vay Vandana Scheme beneficiaries.

2.3.2 Existing AB PM-JAY Families - Shared Top-Up Cover
      For senior citizens aged 70 years and above who belong to families already covered under AB PM-JAY
      (hereinafter referred to as “existing families”), an additional shared top-up cover of up to Rs. 5.00 lakh
      per year shall be provided, subject to the following conditions:
       a.   For existing families, the additional per family cost per year is estimated to be Rs. 75.70 for the
            additional top up wallet of Rs.5 Lakh which will be utilized only when the primary family wallet of
            Rs. 5 lakh is fully exhausted.

      a.    This cover shall not be available to other members of the new family who are not of the age of 70

      a.    The beneficiary must declare that they are not receiving free healthcare services under any other

4. GIA FOR IMPLEMENTATION
4.1     Modes of Implementation
4.1.1 States and Union Territories may implement AB PM-JAY through one of the following three modes:
        a.     Insurance Mode: The State or UT implements AB PM-JAY through an Insurance Company, without
               which the policy period does not commence.
        b.     Trust Mode: The State or UT implements AB PM-JAY through its designated Trust, Society, or
               Organisation without the involvement of an Insurance Company.
        c.     Mixed/Hybrid Mode: The State or UT implements AB PM-JAY through a combination of Insurance
               Mode and Trust Mode.
4.2     Conditions for Release of GIA
4.2.1 The release of Central Government’s Share of GIA shall be subject to the following conditions:
        a.     The State or UT shall release its proportionate share upfront into the separate designated escrow
               account of SHA opened for the implementation of AB PM-JAY and share the details of funds released
               along with a copy of the bank statement with NHA.
        b.     The State or UT shall also share the Utilisation Certificate in Form 12-A of the General Financial
               Rules 2017, and copies of Annual Audited Statement of Accounts along with any other requisite
               documents specified by NHA from time to time.
        c.     For the purpose of deciding the annual tenure for release of GIA, the period shall be the Financial
               Year, i.e., from 1st April to 31st March. Computation of Central share of contribution shall be based
               on 365 days, or 366 days in the case of a leap year, and not on a monthly basis.
4.3     Schedule of Release
4.3.1 Insurance Mode: For States and UTs implementing AB PM-JAY in Insurance Mode, and for the Insurance
      component of Mixed Mode, the GIA shall be released as per the timeline decided in the contract signed
      between the SHA and the Insurance Company.
4.3.2 Trust Mode: For States and UTs implementing AB PM-JAY in Trust Mode, Grant-in-Aid (GIA) shall be
      released, preferably in two tranches of 50% each as per the requirements raised by the respective states,


                                                                   36
      subject to the proportionate upfront release of the State share and the utilization certificate of earlier
      released GIA.
4.3.3 Mixed Mode: The annual payable Grant-in-Aid in case of Mixed Mode, will be funds will be computed
      for the premium payment as per the applicable insurance contract signed between SHA and insurance
      company. Further, the computation of payable treatment cost of NHA’s Share for claim payment under
      trust mode will be done from the left-over funds with revised beneficiary base.


5. GIA FOR ADMINISTRATION
5.1   GIA Admin under AB PM-JAY
5.1.1 Grant-in-Aid for Administrative Expenses (GIA-Admin) is the financial assistance provided to
      administrative costs incurred in the course of implementation of AB PM-JAY, irrespective all modes of
      implementation.
5.2   Utilisation of GIA for Administrative Expenses
5.2.1 The GIA released for Administrative Expenses shall be utilised only for the specific purpose of incurring
      administrative expenses in the course of implementation of AB PM-JAY by the SHA. Expenditure shall be
      incurred in a manner so that there is no rush of expenditure in February and March of any financial year.
5.3   Permissible Heads of Expenditure
5.3.1 The types of expenses permitted to be incurred under the Administrative GIA are set out below. The
      percentages indicated against each head are indicative, as prescribed under the Guidelines for Release
      of Administrative Expenses. These indicative percentages stand modified by the Office Memorandum
      No. S-120/18/277/2022-NHA dated 23rd February 2026 attached as Annexure 12.
5.4   Flexible Heads (No Fixed Ceiling)
5.4.1 Human Resources
      a.    Payment to contractual staff, either recruited directly or through an agency, for administrative
            work specific to AB PM-JAY.
      b.    Travel expenses incurred specifically for work related to AB PM-JAY; travel, lodging, and boarding
            shall be regulated as per the respective State’s or UT’s rules.
      c.    Holding of training programmes.
5.4.2 IT Infrastructure
      a.    IT-related purchases specifically for AB PM-JAY work, including hardware such as computers,
            peripherals, and printers.
      b.    Software applications required for administrative work, such as MS Office suite or Adobe
            applications.
      c.    AMC charges and other associated and incidental expenses.
5.4.3 IEC (Information, Education, and Communication) – Minimum 10%
      a.    Advertising and promotion.
      b.    Printing of brochures and promotional material in local languages.
      c.    Translation and delivery of scheme-related information and material.
      d.    Beneficiary awareness initiatives, outreach campaigns, capacity-building activities, media
            engagement, and dissemination of scheme-related information.

5.5   Restricted Heads (Max 15% Combined)
5.5.1 Office Expenses
       a.   Hiring of vehicles specifically for AB PM-JAY work.
       b.   Newspaper bills.
       c.   Subscriptions to magazines pertaining to the Health Sector.
       d.   Payment of electricity and water bills.
       e.   Bank charges levied on the SHA by their bank.
       f.   Purchase of office supplies and postage specifically for AB PM-JAY work.
       g.   Phone bills and internet bills as per applicable norms.
       h.   Lease rent of office space of SHA.
       i.   Payment of monthly rent for rooms/buildings.
       j.   Rental of office equipment specifically for AB PM-JAY work.
5.5.2 Contingencies: Any Unforeseen expenditure incurred for the implementation of AB PM-JAY.
5.6   Stage of Release of GIA for Administrative Expenses
5.6.1 The GIA for Administrative Expenses shall be released as per the requirements raised by the respective
      states, subject to proportionate upfront release of the State’s or UT’s share and utilisation details of GIA
      released earlier, if any. This schedule of release shall be applicable irrespective of the mode in which AB
      PM-JAY is being implemented in the State.


6. BANKING OPERATIONS AND PAYMENT MODALITIES
6.1   Escrow Account
6.1.1 The State Governments and Union Territories shall open two separate designated escrow accounts, name
      as “GIA Implementation Account” and “GIA Administrative Account”, with any of the banks permissible
      as per the instructions issued by the Ministry of Finance. (Escrow Guidelines attached as Annexure 1)
6.1.2 The escrow accounts shall be opened in accordance with the instructions issued by the Ministry of
      Finance vide O.M. No. S-11012/3(1)/Bank/Ref.Case/2010/RBD/1688-1772 dated 10th November
      2016 (enclosed as Annexure 14), read with O.M. No. S-11012/3(1)/Bank/Ref.Case/2010/GBA/1351-
      1454 dated 21st August 2019 (enclosed as Annexure 15).
6.1.3 The SHA shall share the details of the designated escrow accounts with NHA within 7 days of opening
      the accounts or any change in the details of the designated escrow accounts.
6.2   Flow of Funds
6.2.1 The State or UT shall upfront release its share for each beneficiary category into designated escrow
      accounts of SHA opened for the AB PM-JAY and share the details of funds released along with a copy of
      the bank statement with NHA.
6.2.2 The Central Government shall then release its share of GIA through the PFMS portal into the designated
      escrow accounts of the SHA of the respective State or UT (as applicable) within 21 working days from
      the receipt of the duly completed proposal from the State Government.
6.2.3 In case of Insurance mode, upon receipt of the Central Government’s Share of GIA, the State or UT shall
      release the aforesaid instalment of premium within 7 working days from the SHA Premium Escrow
      Account to the Insurance Company. The complete fund flow of the scheme requires regular flow of
      information and monitoring of funds at all levels. At the State/UT level, SHA, shall ensure the timely

           disbursement of funds to downward agencies that is from the bank to the EHCPs.
6.3        Technology and Monitoring Requirements:
6.3.1 SHA shall ensure selection of a bank which has robust technology solutions with experience of handling
      government transactions including providing escrow arrangement services for government schemes
      and projects. The solution provided by SHA’s banker shall facilitate the following:
           a.   Multi-level fund monitoring through a single account system,
           b.   Authorization-based payment mechanism in the form of Maker and Checker,
           c.   Component head-wise payment and tracking - received funds categorised in the form of expenditure
                heads,
           d.   Real-time balance reports to monitor all expenditure head-wise transaction summaries,
           e.   View access of transactions across hierarchy,
           f.   Customised MIS/Reports to ensure smooth reconciliations,
           g.   Capability of integration with accounting system for easy bookkeeping

8. SPECIAL PROVISIONS
8.1        Refund of Premium - Insurance Mode
8.1.1 In cases where States and Union Territories implement the scheme under Insurance Mode or Mixed


                                                          40
      Mode, the following provisions shall apply:
       a.   The SHA shall ensure that the refund due from the Insurance Company is recovered from them
            within the stipulated time period as mentioned in the contract executed with the Insurance
            Company. Company. SHA shall impose relevant provisions in case of delay on the part of the
            Insurance Company in refund of premium after calculation of the total amount payable to the
            Insurance Company as per the provisions of the contract signed by the States/UTs.
       b.   The amount of refund shall be shared between Centre and State/UT as per the following formulas:
            •   Step 1 - Effective Premium:
                Effective Premium = [(Total premium paid – Total premium returned) / Total Number of
                families]
            •   Step 2 - Per Family Central Share in Effective Premium:
                i.    If Effective Premium < Rs. 1,052: Central Share = Effective Premium × 0.6/0.9/1 (as
                      applicable)
                ii.   If Effective Premium > Rs. 1,052: Central Share = Rs. 1,052 × 0.6/0.9/1 (as applicable)
            •   Step 3 - Per Family State Share in Effective Premium:
                i.    If Effective Premium < Rs. 1,052: State Share = Effective Premium × 0.4/0.1/0 (as
                      applicable)
                ii.   If Effective Premium > Rs. 1,052: State Share = (Rs. 1,052 × 0.4/0.1/0) + (Effective
                      Premium – Rs. 1,052)
            •   Step 4 - Calculation of Central and State Share of Refunds:
                i.    Central Share per family:
                      a. If Effective Premium < Rs. 1,052: Central share in refund = Actual amount paid by
                         NHA – Effective Central Share
                      b. If Effective Premium > Rs. 1,052: Central share in refund = Nil
                ii.   State Share per family:
                      a. If Effective Premium < Rs. 1,052: State share in refund = Actual amount paid by SHA
                         – Effective State Share
                      b. If Effective Premium > Rs. 1,052: State share in refund = Total refund received
                      c. On receipt of the refunds from the Insurance Company, SHA shall calculate the central
                         share of refund and share the information with NHA along with the subsequent
                         proposal for funds release. The central share of refunds received shall be adjusted
                         against the subsequent release of central share.
                      d. In case of Insurance Mode, the due amount of refund shall be shared in the same
                         ratio of premium contributed by NHA and SHA respectively.
