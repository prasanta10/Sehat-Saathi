          National Health Authority (NHA) plays a pivotal role in strengthening India’s health
          financing and service delivery ecosystem through the implementation and stewardship
          of key national health initiatives, most notably Ayushman Bharat Pradhan Mantri Jan
          Arogya Yojana (AB PM-JAY) and the Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission (ABDM). Over the
          years, as an attached office of the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare, NHA has issued
          a wide range of operational guidelines, policy advisories, standard operating procedures,
          and implementation frameworks to support States, Union Territories, insurers, hospitals,
          and other stakeholders in ensuring the effective and uniform implementation of these
          programmes across the country.

          Given the scale and complexity of India’s health system, with diverse administrative
          capacities, institutional arrangements, and service delivery contexts across States and
          Union Territories, it has become increasingly important to consolidate these directives
          into a single, accessible reference resource.
PREFACE




          This Compendium of Guidelines for AB PM-JAY has therefore been developed as a
          comprehensive repository of key guidelines issued by NHA, bringing together critical
          policy documents and operational instructions related to implementation of PM-
          JAY in one place. The compendium aims to enhance clarity, promote consistency in
          implementation, and facilitate easier access to authoritative guidance for all stakeholders
          involved in the execution of the scheme.

          The compendium will serve multiple purposes. First, it will act as a central reference
          document for State Health Agencies, implementation support agencies, insurers, third-
          party administrators, empanelled hospitals, and partner organizations for PM-JAY. By
          consolidating guidelines that were previously issued across different time periods and
          formats, it will help users navigate the regulatory and operational framework more
          efficiently. Second, it contributes to institutional memory and knowledge management
          within the health system by systematically documenting the evolution of policies,
          processes, and operational standards developed by NHA. Third, it supports capacity
          building and training efforts, enabling stakeholders at national, state, and district levels
          to better understand the programmatic requirements and implementation protocols.

          The guidelines included in this compendium span a wide range of thematic areas
          essential to the effective functioning of PM-JAY scheme. These include policies related
          to beneficiary identification and verification, hospital empanelment and management,
          claims management and payment processes, fraud prevention and detection, grievance
          redressal mechanisms, IEC, finance and capacity-building initiatives. Together, these
          guidelines provide a structured framework that ensures transparency, accountability,
          and quality in programme implementation while safeguarding beneficiary interests.

Ayushman Bharat Pradhan Mantri Jan Arogya Yojana (AB PM-JAY) was launched by the Hon’ble Prime Minister
of India, Shri Narendra Modi on 23rd September 2018 in Ranchi, Jharkhand. PM-JAY is the largest health
assurance scheme in the world which aims at providing a health cover of Rs. 5 lakhs per family per year for
secondary and tertiary care hospitalisation.


1. Introduction
1.1 About AB PM-JAY
AB PM-JAY aims to reduce catastrophic health expenditure, improve access to quality healthcare, reduce unmet
healthcare needs, and lower out-of-pocket expenditure for poor and vulnerable families. The scheme initially
covered eligible rural and urban households identified primarily through the Socio-Economic Caste Census
(SECC) 2011 database, including specified deprivation categories in rural areas and identified occupational
categories in urban areas , along with eligible RSBY beneficiary families not reflected in the SECC database.
Subsequently, in March 2024, the beneficiary base was expanded to include families of Accredited Social Health
Activists (ASHAs), field-level health functionaries, Anganwadi Workers (AWWs), and Anganwadi Helpers
(AWHs), and later, in October 2024, senior citizens aged 70 years and above
The scheme initially covered 10.74 crore families and was later expanded to 12 crore families in 2022,
accounting for 11.7% decadal growth rate. States were also given flexibility to use other databases of similar
socioeconomic profile for verification of beneficiaries who could not be identified and verified through the
SECC database. In March 2024, 37.19 lakh families of ASHAs, AWWs, and AWHs were added into the beneficiary
base. In October 2024, the coverage was further extended to 4.5 crore families of senior citizens of age 70 years
and above. Accordingly, the total beneficiary base under AB PM-JAY stood at 15.14 crore families as of FY
2024-25 (Figure 1 below). AB PM-JAY is fully funded by the Government with cost of implementation shared
between the Central and State Governments.

1.2 Objectives of AB PM-JAY Scheme
The objective of Ayushman Bharat Pradhan Mantri Jan Arogya Yojana is to reduce catastrophic health
expenditure, improve access to quality healthcare, reduce unmet healthcare needs, and lower out-of-pocket
expenditure for eligible beneficiary families. Under the scheme, eligible AB PM-JAY beneficiary families are
entitled to cashless coverage for secondary, tertiary, and day-care procedures, as applicable, through the
network of Empanelled Health Care Providers (EHCPs).
1.3 Benefits under AB PM-JAY
The benefits of INR 5,00,000 per annum are on a family floater basis which means that same can be used by
one or all members of the family. AB PM-JAY has been designed in such a way that there is no cap on family size
or age of members. In addition, pre-existing diseases are covered from the very first day. The cover under the
scheme includes all expenses incurred on the following components of the treatment:
   1.   Medical examination, Inpatient treatment and consultation
   2.   Day care procedures
   3.   Pre-hospitalisation up to 3 days
   4.   Medicine and medical consumables
   5.   Non-intensive and intensive care services
   6.   Diagnostic and laboratory investigations
   7.   Medical implantation services (where necessary)
   8.   Food services
   9.   Complications arising during treatment
   10. Post-hospitalization follow-up care up to 15 days
AB PM-JAY remains a cashless scheme, but co-payment may be permitted in limited state-enabled situations
where the beneficiary wallet is insufficient or the package itself exceeds the wallet limit, subject to informed
consent, platform-based processing, and policy controls (More details in section 8.7 below).

health care providers. As on 31st December 2025, there were 32,574 empanelled public & private hospitals
that met defined quality and infrastructure criteria.
Unit of coverage under the Scheme is an entitled household, referred to as an AB PM-JAY Beneficiary Family
Unit, which comprises all individuals mapped to that household in the PM-JAY beneficiary database. A unit
includes the head of the household and all listed family members (as recorded in the beneficiary list), and
there is no cap on family size, age, or gender. Any change in the unit composition is permitted only through the
Government-approved beneficiary update process. Under the ‘Add Member’ provisions, addition is allowed
only to an already eligible household, subject to prescribed documentary proof and timelines. The presence
of a person’s name in the verified beneficiary list (as updated from time to time) serve as proof of eligibility of
the Beneficiary Family Unit for availing benefits under the Scheme.
No entry or exit age restrictions apply to the members of a Beneficiary Family Unit; and no member of a
Beneficiary Family Unit are required to undergo a pre-insurance health check-up or medical examination
before their eligibility as a beneficiary and all pre-existing illnesses of the beneficiaries are covered.
1.4 Health Benefit Packages (HBPs)
Health Benefit Packages (HBPs) under AB PM-JAY are a defined set of covered healthcare services, medical
and surgical treatments, and corresponding package rates made available to eligible beneficiaries on a
cashless basis through EHCPs, subject to the provisions of the scheme. HBPs include inpatient hospitalisation
and day-care treatment, along with associated diagnostics, medicines, implants, procedures, pre- and post-
hospitalisation care, follow-up care, and other treatment-related services.
HBPs may be broadly classified into two categories: medical and surgical. Medical HBPs include both fixed-
cost packages and per-day medical management packages. Fixed-cost medical packages include those under
specialties such as Medical Oncology, Radiation Oncology, and Interventional Radiology, while per-day medical
management packages include those under specialties such as General Medicine. In contrast, all surgical
packages are fixed-cost packages.
Since the scheme’s inception, five versions of HBPs have been released under AB PM-JAY as part of an on-going
rationalisation process. These versions are HBP 1.0, 2.0, 2.1, 2.2, and 2022. HBP Guidelines and Packages are
updated time to time and are available on the AB PM-JAY website.

   k.   Any other expenses related to the treatment of the patient in the hospital.

B. Salient features of HBP
1.   HBPs are package-based entitlements for inpatient and day-care treatment, and the package rate is a
     comprehensive rate applicable for the policy period. It includes treatment-related services such as
     consultation, bed charges, medicines, diagnostics, implants where applicable, pre-hospitalisation, and
     post-hospitalisation/follow-up care up to 15 days for the same ailment or surgery, unless specifically
     provided otherwise.
2.   HBPs are of two broad types: medical and surgical and adjudication must distinguish between fixed-rate
     packages and per-day medical management packages. Surgical packages are fixed-rate; medical packages
     may be fixed-rate or per-day depending on specialty and package type.
3.   From HBP 2.0 onward, packages and procedures are distinct, and the final booking/selection is always at
     the procedure level. For adjudication, this means the selected procedure, not merely the package name,
     must match the diagnosis, clinical condition, approach, and documentation.
4.   Procedure selection must conform to the patient’s actual clinical condition because HBP 2.0 onward is
     far more granular than HBP 1.0. Therefore it is important to verify that the exact procedure chosen is
     clinically justified and not just broadly related to the package title.
5.   Stratification is a key adjudication determinant. Certain procedures are stratified by factors such as type
     of anaesthesia, surgical technique, laterality, age, gender, aetiology, or other cost-driving differences. Any
     selected stratification must be supported by clinical records because it directly affects price.
6.   Per-day medical management packages are stratified by bed category and paid on the basis of rate ×
     number of bed-days. The bed category selected at admission is therefore an adjudication-critical input,
     and per-day medical package payment must align with ward/HDU/ICU category and actual length of stay.
7.   Cross-specialty packages should be recognised as a salient HBP feature. These packages appear under
     more than one specialty but retain the same code and price. This is important in adjudication to prevent
     duplicate interpretation or specialty-based upcoding for the same procedure.
8.   Add-on packages are permissible only when mapped to a primary package, and valid add-ons should not
     attract deduction merely because they are additional. They do not alter the identity or base price of the
     primary package.
9.   High-end diagnostic/investigation add-ons are not standalone packages. They are payable only with
     mapped primary medical management packages, subject to pre-authorisation and the applicable cap,
     and surgical packages are generally all-inclusive unless specific add-ons are provided in the HBP master.
10. Standalone packages are those packages that cannot be claimed as enhancements and cannot be clubbed
    with other existing or unspecified surgical packages. This is directly relevant for adjudication because
    clubbing such procedures would be inadmissible.
11. Follow-up packages are separately recognised for selected procedures and may be used beyond the
    standard 15-day follow-up of the original package only with clear documentary support. Adjudication
    should require proof of the parent/original procedure and documentation of the follow-up need and
    activities.
12. Abuse-prone packages require enhanced scrutiny. These are flagged in TMS, may trigger NAFU alerts, and
    are subject to closer monitoring, audit, and investigation.
13. Public-reserved packages must be adjudicated against the applicable reservation status. Certain packages/
    procedures are reserved for public EHCPs, and states may modify the reservation list subject to policy




                                                        27
provisions. A list of such packages and procedures approved at the NHA level is given in Annexure 2.
14. Emergency packages may be auto-approved and do not require routine Pre-authorisation Processing
    Doctor (PPD) approval. Further, in the case of per-day medical management packages, the first day of pre-
    authorisation is automatically approved by the system. However, any subsequent enhancement request
    for extended hospitalisation is routed to the PPD for review and approval. In such cases, the Medical
    Coordinator (MEDCO) may submit enhancement requests for a maximum of two additional days for
    private EHCPs and five additional days for public EHCPs. For example, ‘Hydrocephalus’ under the General
    Medicine specialty. The HBP guidelines also notes that 97 procedures continue under auto-approval, 429
    were removed from auto-approval, and 120 per-day medical management procedures retain day-1 auto-
    approval.
15. Combination rules for booking multiple packages are adjudication-critical and should be fully stated.
    Surgical-surgical combinations follow the 100%-50%-25% rule (Details in section 4.5.6 below); surgical
    + non-per-day medical combinations are payable in full for both; medical + medical combinations other
    than per-day medical packages are payable in full for both; per-day medical management packages cannot
    be clubbed together; and surgical + per-day medical combinations are ordinarily not allowed except by
    specific SHA-supported exception.
16. Unspecified Surgical Packages (USPs) are meant only for surgical treatments/procedures not available
    in the applicable HBP master and only in exceptional or life-saving situations. They are not for medical
    management, excluded services, government-reserved packages booked by private hospitals, individual
    drugs/diagnostics, or cosmetic/aesthetic care. USP approval flow and payment rule are critical
    adjudication features. USP requires compulsory pre-authorisation, cannot be booked under multiple
    package selection, is examined by the SHA Medical Committee and approved by the CEO, SHA, must be
    decided within 48 hours, and the approved amount usually based on the closest matching surgery/rate
    becomes final; CPD should not later deduct or partially approve it (More details in section 8.4 below).
17. Final Procedure Price = Procedure price + Stratification rate (if any) + Incentive (if applicable) + Implant(s)
    rate (if any). This is a core adjudication principle. Incentive rules are given in Annexure 3. Incentives apply
    only on the procedure rate and not on the implant component; only one quality-related incentive may be
    availed at a time; and applicable incentives are compounded, not added arithmetically. Under HBP 2022
    differential pricing, rates may vary by city tier and, as applicable under the HBP design, by hospital type/
    teaching-hospital adjustment. Adjudication must therefore use the correct configured price basis for the
    applicable state/version. Implant cost is treated separately in the system, and payment may vary by type
    and number of implants. This is important for claims because implant-linked procedures need scrutiny of
    the selected implant category and quantity, including age- or gender-appropriate device selection where
    relevant. Step payment of staged procedures is a distinct HBP feature. Certain procedures are split into
    separately payable stages, and the treating physician may need to specify the stage being undertaken.
    This affects both admissibility and amount payable (More details in section 5 of HBP guidelines).
18. Co-payment is an exception, not the norm. AB PM-JAY remains a cashless scheme, but co-payment may be
    permitted in limited state-enabled situations where the beneficiary wallet is insufficient or the package
    itself exceeds the wallet limit, subject to informed consent, platform-based processing, and policy controls
    (More details in section 8.7 below).
19. As per OM no. S-12012/183/2022-NHA (Pt.1), dated 01 February 2022 (Annexure 4), the Governing
    Board of NHA has approved the proposal to give flexibility to States/UTs in deciding on design and cost
    of health benefit packages under AB PM-JAY. This flexibility essentially means that till the time packages
    are not in AB PM-JAY exclusion category state has the flexibility to modify the packages. NHA shall share
    the financial burden as per mutual arrangement, subject to adherence to ceiling limits defined under the
    scheme.


                                                        28
20. States/UTs may increase package prices by up to 10% over the NHA-configured package rates. However,
    there shall be no limit on reduction of the calibrated package rates. The additional financial burden for
    package priced beyond 110% of the NHA recommended price would be borne exclusively by the State/
    UT with no additional contribution from NHA. More details on state/UT’s customisation of HBPs may be
    referred to HBP guidelines.
21. Standard Treatment Guidelines (STGs) are integral to HBP implementation. STGs are embedded in TMS,
    package-specific mandatory documents are rationalised through them, and claims adjudication is expected
    to interpret package admissibility and documentation in line with the applicable STG requirements.
22. NHA does not define a universal minimum or maximum length of stay for packages. This is a useful
    adjudication principle to mention because LOS must be judged against clinical justification and package
    rules, not assumed from a fixed national minimum/maximum unless specifically provided.
1.5 Implementation Arrangements
    The scheme has a three-tier implementation structure with dedicated institutional bodies at the National,
    State, and district level to manage the implementation of AB PM-JAY across the country
    A. National Health Authority (NHA)
    National Health Authority (NHA) is the apex body responsible for implementing India’s flagship public
    health insurance/assurance scheme AB PM-JAY. National Health Authority was previously functioning as
    a registered society National Health Agency, which was functioning as a registered society since 23rd May
    2018. Pursuant to Cabinet decision for full functional autonomy, National Health Agency was reconstituted
    as the National Health Authority on 2nd January 2019, under Gazette Notification Registered No. DL –(N)
    04/0007/2003-18.
    B. State Health Agencies
    SHA is a nodal agency responsible for implementation of AB PM-JAY in the State/UT is headed by
    CEO. SHA can hire required additional staff, engage Implementation Support Agency (ISA)/Insurer for
    implementation of the scheme. CEO, SHA is appointed by the State government and is ex-officio member
    secretary of the Governing Council of the SHA. Along with day-to-day operations of implementation of
    AB PM-JAY in the state, SHA is also responsible for data sharing, verification and validation of family
    members, IEC, monitoring of the scheme etc.
    C. District Implementation Unit
    In addition to the SHA, a District Implementation Unit (DIU) has also been established to support
    implementation at the district level. The DIU is chaired by the Deputy Commissioner/District Magistrate/
    Collector of the district and is responsible for coordinating between various stakeholders at the grass-
    roots level.
    At empaneled hospital level, Pradhan Mantri Arogya Mitras (PMAMs) are stationed to assist beneficiaries
    in verifying their eligibility, generating e-cards, guiding them through pre-authorization and claims
    processes, and ensuring patient satisfaction. The scheme is currently being implemented in 35 States/UTs
    (barring West Bengal). There are three models of scheme implementation. States and Union Territories
    have the flexibility to choose their preferred mode of implementation.
    •    Trust Mode: Adopted by 27 states, this model is directly implemented by the State Health Agency
         (SHA). In this arrangement, the SHA processes and manages claims either in-house or through an
         Implementation Support Agency (ISA), and hospital payments are made directly by the SHA.
    •    Insurance Mode: Implemented by 6 states (Rajasthan, Tamil Nadu, Dadra Nagar Haveli & Daman and
         Diu, Meghalaya, Lakshadweep, and Nagaland), where the SHA selects an Insurance Company (IC)
         through a tendering process to manage the scheme. The SHA pays the agreed premium to the IC, and

                                                     29
       the IC (directly or through its TPA) processes pre-authorizations/claims on the TMS platform and
            reimburses empanelled hospitals by transferring payments to their bank accounts. In the insurance
            model, empanelled hospitals generally operate under a tripartite arrangement (SHA–IC–Hospital),
            and payment delays attributable to the insurer (and not due to premium payment delays by SHA)
            may attract penal interest payable to hospitals. For portability, the insurer is expected to reimburse
            the treating hospital at the package rates applicable in the hospital state, with portability liability
            factored into the quoted premium.
       •    Hybrid Mode: Used by 2 states (Gujarat and Jharkhand), this model combines both trust and insurance
            modes. The insurance company provides initial coverage up to a certain amount, with the remaining
            coverage up to ₹5 lakhs managed under the trust mode.
       The entire scheme is implemented with support from a robust end-to-end digital platform that enables
       real-time tracking of patient eligibility, service utilisation, claims processing, fund disbursement, and
       monitoring. This digital infrastructure is now being used also for implementation of health schemes by
       other Ministries such as MoRTH/CGHS/CAPF among others. Quality of care is ensured through a quality
       control mechanism focusing on norms for empanelment, standard treatment guidelines, accreditation
       incentives (NABH), grading of facilities, capacity building, etc.

3. Basics of claims adjudication

 Claims adjudication refers to the decision on two key aspects of a claim: whether the claim is admissible
 under the terms of policy/Scheme and if yes, what is the quantum payable. It applies to the final decision on
 claims payment. The decision involves cross verification of all-important aspects – covered person, medical
 condition – symptoms, diagnosis, treatment, policy exclusions, period, available sum insured, pre-agreed
 tariff/package rate, empanelled hospital etc.
 In PMJAY, claim adjudication is done through integrated workflows between three key systems – Beneficiary
 Identification System (BIS), Transaction Management System (TMS) and Hospital Engagement Module
 (HEM). The key tasks are performed under Transaction Management System (TMS), partially at the time
 of Pre-authorization by Pre-authorization Processing Doctor (PPD) and later at the time of final claim
 settlement by Claim Processing Doctor (CPD) based on the documents received from hospital.
 While processing a pre-auth or adjudicating a claim, it is important to take utmost care and be mindful of
 the decision because any wrong adjudication may lead to inconvenience to beneficiaries or recoveries from
 hospital/ISA/Insurer at a later stage.
 The system under AB PM-JAY is designed to help the claims processing team adjudicate claim end to end,
 however human intelligence needs to be applied while processing/approving both Pre- authorization and
 claims. Below mentioned points should be kept in mind while processing a pre-auth or a claim, any of these
 are auto verified at the system level through inbuilt algorithms and rules:
 1.   The patient should be an eligible beneficiary and verified through Beneficiary Identification System
      (BIS) or state managed beneficiary database via TMS.
 2.   The treatment package claimed should be covered under the Scheme and should comply with the
      state-specific reservation in the package masters, if any
 3.   The conditions should not fall under the exclusion (Annexure 5) criteria as defined under the policy
 4.   The available sum insured in the beneficiary’s family wallet should be enough for payment of the
      current treatment
 5.   The processing team should ensure that all the documents submitted by the EHCP confirm that
      admission/ Hospitalization was necessary.
 6.   The processing team should validate all the details/ information (patient details, diagnosis details,
      supporting investigation documents, treatment chosen) submitted at the time of Pre-auth and highlight
      discrepancy if any.
 7.   The processing team should raise a query only in case of any missing information mandatory to process
      a claim or in cases of non-compliance with STG.
 8.   The processing team should make an informed and mindful decision on the payment to be made to the
      EHCP.
 9.   The claim approved amount should not be more than the Pre-auth approved amount and the wallet
      balance.
 At the time of Claim Submission, EHCP must submit:
 1.   Discharge summary as per the NHA prescribed format (Annexure 6), death summary if applicable.
 2.   Package specific mandatory documents as per STGs as defined in the TMS.
 However, if required for medical audit/checks/quality assurance, all medical records of the beneficiary
 must be preserved by the EHCP and made available on being asked at the time of audit.

4.1 System Overview
  In AB PM-JAY, the three core IT systems work together to ensure that eligible beneficiaries receive cashless
  care at empanelled hospitals, and that every claim is recorded and paid correctly.

The Beneficiary Identification System (BIS) is the main system used to verify beneficiaries using their
E-card. It stores and confirms beneficiary details.
The Hospital Engagement Module (HEM) is the platform where State Health Agencies (SHAs) receive
and review applications from hospitals that want to empanel under the scheme. SHAs use HEM to approve
or reject these applications. Once approved, the list of empanelled hospitals, along with their profiles and
approved specialties, is published online.
The Transaction Management System (TMS) handles the complete claims process from start to finish.
It includes patient registration, pre-authorization, treatment details, discharge, claim submission and
processing, payments, and dashboards.
The Medical Coordinator (MEDCO) uses TMS to:
   • Verify beneficiaries (by accessing records from BIS)
   • Select and finalize treatment packages
   • Manage approvals, including pre-authorization
In summary, TMS receives beneficiary data from BIS and approved hospital data from HEM and manages
the complete process from patient admission to claim approval and payment.

4.3 Overall Claims Journey Flow
The claim journey starts when the beneficiary presents their PM-JAY and Aadhaar ID at an EHCP, where the
MEDCO completes registration and the patient is taken up for diagnosis and admission. A case number is
generated and the MEDCO initiates a pre-authorisation request, which is reviewed by the pre-auth doctor (PPD)
and marked approved/query/rejected. Once treatment is delivered and the patient is discharged, the MEDCO
updates discharge details along with Mangal Kamna Patra (Feedback form) and submits the claim request
as per mandatory documents enclosed in STGs. After claim is submited by MEDCO, the claim is scrtuinised
via the Claim Executives (Non-technical review) and thoroughly examined by CPDs, with outcomes such as
approval (often with a permissible deduction threshold), or a query back to the hospital. Approved claims flow
to the Accounts Officer, then to the SHA, and finally payment is processed through the bank, with transaction
details reported back to the SHA. If the case attracts rejection >20% of the amount claimed by the hospital, it is
escalated to MAC for review; if still rejected, the hospital can raise a review request to CRC, after which a final
rejection may be issued. However, if there is up to 20% deduction in a claim, the MEDCO can raise a one-time
review request to CPD 2 within TMS 2.0, after which it is decided by CPD-2 to either reject or approve it. Figure

Priority of processing portability pre-authorization requests
  Portability pre-authorization requests shall be accorded top priority over other pre-authorization cases
  and processed on a FIFO (First-In, First-Out) basis within the applicable priority category. A single unified
  queue for both portability and intrastate requests is recommended to ensure unbiased processing flow.
  The priority sequence for processing pre-authorization requests shall be as follows:
      1. Emergency Portability preauthorization.
      2. Emergency Intrastate preauthorization.
      3. Planned Portability preauthorization.
      4. Planned Intrastate Preauthorization.
  A maximum TAT of 6 hours shall be strictly adhered to for processing portability pre-authorization requests.
  In the event the stipulated TAT is breached, the request shall be forced-approved by the system, irrespective
  of the time of day, as configured in TMS.

4.8 Recovery of Excess or Wrong Payment or Penalty to EHCP
  Recovery may be initiated when (i) excess payment has been made against a settled claim, (ii) a claim has
  been wrongly paid to an EHCP, or (iii) a penalty is to be recovered. Recoveries may be processed within the
  same state or from the hospital’s State in portability cases. The ACO initiates the recovery in the system,
  after which the SHA may approve, reject, or raise a query (queries are routed back to the ACO). Upon
  SHA approval, the system triggers the recovery and adjusts the recoverable amount against the EHCP’s
  subsequent claims until the full amount is recovered.
  Recoveries are of two types: hospital-level and case-level. Hospital-level recovery is a lump-sum recovery
  from a hospital and is typically used for same-state cases. Case-level recovery is linked to a specific case
  where excess/wrong payment occurred and is generally used for portability recoveries from the Hospital
  State.

8. Payment in Special Cases
  Once a patient is admitted under Ayushman Bharat Pradhan Mantri Jan Arogya Yojana (PM-JAY) in an
  empanelled hospital, in normal course the patient will be discharged by the hospital after completing the
  treatment. Hence in majority of cases, the payment to the hospital will be done based on the package booked
  and rates prescribed for that package.

                                                          57
  However, in some cases, this may not happen due to various reasons e.g. patient may leave against medical
  advice, patient may die within the hospital or patient may need to be referred to another hospital.
  In these special cases clarity needs to be provided to both hospitals and the payers (State Health Agency/
  Insurance Company) regarding payments to the hospitals.
  This guideline provide details of payments to be done in these special cases. The basic principles to be
  followed in implementation of these guidelines are as follows:
  •    The hospital will be paid partial amount only if the hospital provides information about deviation from
       normal course to the respective SHA/ISA/Insurer through the IT platform as soon as possible but not
       later than 24 hours of the deviation. The time limit may be relaxed to 72 hours for public hospitals
  •    Additionally, in each of these cases payment will be done only after a successful audit by the SHA /
       Insurer.
  •    The audit process shall be completed by the SHA/ Insurer within 15 days of receiving the information
       from the hospital.
  •    It is expected that these deviations would not amount to more than 5% in a particular hospital.
8.1 LAMA/DAMA
  Leave Against Medical Advice (LAMA) and Discharge Against Medical Advice (DAMA) are related but not
  identical terms and are often used interchangeably in practice. In general, both refer to a situation in which
  a patient leaves the hospital before the treating physician recommends discharge. LAMA usually refers
  to the patient leaving the hospital on their own request (or at the request of the family/attendant), while
  DAMA refers to the discharge being formally documented as being against medical advice. Such events
  may occur due to reasons related to beneficiary, family, hospital circumstances, or other administrative/
  financial factors.

8.4 Unspecified Surgical Package
  To ensure that AB PM-JAY beneficiaries are not denied necessary care, a provision for Unspecified Surgical
  Packages (USPs) has been made for surgical treatments/procedures that are not available in the applicable
  national HBP version. Further, to expedite the approval process of USP and provide more flexibility to
  states/UTs, the Governing Board of NHA decided to delegate the authority to approve requests for booking
  USP up to Rs. 5 lakh to respective SHA (OM No. S-12012/183/2022-NHA(Pt.1), dated 01 February 2022
  (Annexure 17).
8.4.1 Key Aspects of USPs
  An Unspecified Surgical Package (USP) may be considered only where a required surgical treatment or
  procedure is not available in the applicable HBP master and where denial of care would otherwise result.
  Approval of a USP up to ₹5 lakh is delegated to the SHA, subject to examination by the SHA Medical Committee
  and approval by the CEO, SHA. USPs should be used only in exceptional or life-saving circumstances and
  should not be used for medical management cases, excluded treatments, government-reserved packages
  booked by private hospitals, or individual drugs and diagnostics. Some of the key aspects of USPs are given
  in detail below:
      a.     Every USP request shall, before being placed before the competent authority, be examined by the
             SHA Medical Committee. The request, along with the Committee’s opinion, shall then be placed
             before the competent authority for approval. The competent authority for approving USP requests

                                                       61
     up to ₹5 lakh shall be the CEO, SHA. SHAs shall maintain a record of the Medical Committee’s
     opinion and the reasons for approval or rejection recorded by the CEO, SHA, for audit purposes.
b.   SHAs should also frame a policy for inclusion of frequently booked USPs and share a quarterly list
     of approved USPs with paid claims with NHA and the SHA Governing Board for information and
     review. NHA may review the list of approved USPs shared by SHAs for possible inclusion under
     the National or State Package Master.
c.   To book packages under unspecified category, the package should not be listed under the
     respective HBP master.
d.   Benefits under the USP shall be available to the beneficiary on a cashless and paperless basis at
     any Empanelled Health Care Provider (EHCP), subject to the available balance in the beneficiary’s
     AB PM-JAY wallet/sum insured.
e.   Compulsory pre-authorization is in-built while selecting USP code for blocking treatments and
     cannot be raised under multiple package selection.
f.   USP is not applicable for medical management cases. (E.g., General Medicine, Paediatric Medical
     Management, Interventional Radiology, Cardiology, Medical Oncology, Radiation Oncology etc.).
g.   Government reserved packages cannot be availed by private hospitals under USP code. PPD/
     Claims Processing Doctor (CPD) may reject such claims on these grounds. In addition, SHA may
     circulate respective government reserved packages to all empanelled hospitals. Further, states
     need to establish suitable mechanisms to refer such cases to the public system as a means to avoid
     denial of care.
h.   USP cannot be booked for removal of implants that were inserted under the same policy. However,
     in cases where implant removal is not covered under any other package, prior approval may be
     obtained from the State Health Agency or the National Health Authority.
i.   In portability cases, the approval team of the patient’s home State may reject a request where a
     government-reserved package of the patient State has been selected by a private hospital in the
     treating State, unless the case is considered on emergency grounds
j.   Aesthetic treatments of any nature cannot be availed as USP or as such under any other listed
     codes under AB PM-JAY. Only medically necessary with functional purpose/indications can
     be covered. The procedure should result in improving/restoring bodily function or to correct
     significant deformity resulting from accidental injury, trauma or to address congenital anomalies
     that have resulted in significant functional impairment.
k.   Individual drugs or diagnostics cannot be availed under USP code. Only listed drugs and diagnostics
     with fixed price schedules, listed under the drop down of respective specialties, are included for
     blocking treatments.
l.   None of the treatments that fall under the exclusion (Annexure 5) under PM-JAY can be availed
     viz. individual diagnostics for evaluation, out-patient care, drug rehabilitation, cosmetic or
     aesthetic treatments, vaccination, hormone replacement therapy for sex change or any treatment
     related to sex change, any dental treatment or surgery which is corrective, cosmetic or of aesthetic
     procedure, filling of cavity, root canal including wear and tear, etc. unless arising from disease or
     injury and which need hospitalisation for treatment etc. However, for life threatening cases e.g. of
     suicide attempt or accident due to excess consumption of alcohol, treatment shall be provided by
     the hospital till the patient’s condition stabilises.
m.   For deciding the approval amount, the PPD may consider the rate of the closest matching surgery
     available under listed AB PM-JAY packages. The amount approved by the PPD shall be treated as


                                               62
            final, shall be communicated to the hospital, and shall not be subject to subsequent deduction or
            partial approval by the CPD.
      n.    For any state/UT to utilise the USP, it is to be ensured that the same is approved only in (a)
            exceptional circumstances and/or (b) for life saving conditions.
           i.    Exceptional circumstances may include:
                 •    Rare disease conditions or rare surgeries.
                 •    Procedure available under HBP in a different specialty but not available in the treating
                      specialty.
                 •    Procedure available under HBP in a specialty for which the hospital is not empanelled.
                 •    Other conditions treatments which are not excluded under AB PM-JAY but not listed in
                      HBP.
           ii.   Life-saving conditions may include:
                 •    Emergencies or life-threatening conditions
8.4.2 USP Approval
  To expedite the approval process of Unspecified Surgical Packages (USPs) and provide more operational
  flexibility to state/UTs, the Governing Board of NHA decided to delegate the authority to approve requests
  for booking USPs up to 5 lakhs to respective State Health Agency (O.M. No. S-12018/353/2023-NHA, dated

8.7 Co-payment by beneficiaries
  As per O.M. No. S-12012/183/2022-NHA (Pt.I) dated 01st February 2022 (Annexure 21), beneficiaries are
  allowed to make co-payments under AB PM-JAY in case of package costing beyond wallet limit of Rs. 5 Lakhs
  or where beneficiary wallet balance falls short of package cost.
  Context - Under AB PM-JAY, each beneficiary family is entitled for health cover of Rs. 5 lakhs per year. Further,
  EHCPs are paid on the basis of defined health benefit package encompassing all expenditures related to
  availing healthcare benefits under particular procedure. However, many-a-times, beneficiary families were
  not able to use the full wallet amount due to limitations imposed by policy on self-contribution or co-
  payment through any other Central/ State government scheme previously. In case of insufficient balance
  in wallet for package listed under AB PM-JAY, beneficiary was not able to use his/ her wallet for availing
  healthcare services while paying the remaining amount from pocket.
  A beneficiary has an option to use other sources of funding over and above PM-JAY wallet (if required) for
  availing healthcare services in following circumstances:
       a.   through self-contribution in case beneficiary wallet has insufficient balance for availing any
            package listed under AB PM-JAY
       b.   topping the beneficiary wallet by availing beneficiary entitlement under some other scheme i.e.
            Rashtriya Arogya Nidhi (RAN) / Health Ministers’ Discretionary Grant (HMDG) or other Central
            or State sponsored scheme. Several States/UTs have implemented schemes with provision of
            higher funding for rare/life threatening diseases.
       c.   through self-contribution in case of such package where cost of package itself is higher than Rs. 5
            lakhs e.g., lungs transplant.
  To avoid any abuse of co-payment provision, following checks and balances are being put in place:
       a.   All such requests should be processed on AB PM-JAY IT platform.
       b.   Copayment shall be triggered only in cases where the beneficiary wallet is insufficient for booking
            any package and there is a willingness on part of beneficiary to make the additional payments or
            it can be topped up through some other Central/State scheme wallet.
       c.   For availing co-payment option, hospital and beneficiary consent (Consent Forms - Annexures
            22 and 23) shall be mandatory, which shall have to be recorded in writing duly signed by the
            beneficiary or his/her guardian. Such consent must be uploaded on IT platform and preserved for
            audit purpose.
       d.   CEO, SHA shall be the competent authority to approve any request for availing funding from
            other sources including from beneficiary pocket. In case of using other scheme wallet, decision of
            CEO, SHA has to be subsequently concurred by the concerned authority (if any) responsible for
            approvals under another scheme.
       e.   Co-payment option will not be available for unspecified procedures or for packages under
            exclusion category of NHA/SHA.
       f.   All such cases will be mandatorily audited by NAFU. Details of such audit report must be presented
            during the subsequent Executive Committee/Governing Board meetings of NHA and respective
            SHA.
       g.   NHA will issue guidelines for taking punitive action against EHCPs found to have coerced
            beneficiary for co-payment or misused the option of availing additional co-funding from another
            scheme.

9. Recoveries and other Actions Post Confirmation of
Fraud and other Irregularities
 AB PM-JAY is being implemented with a zero-tolerance approach towards any kind of fraud, covering entire
 gamut of activities for prevention, detection, and deterrence of different kinds of fraud that could occur in
 PM-JAY at different stages of its implementation.
 This guideline: “Guidelines for recoveries and other actions post confirmation of fraud and other
 irregularities” would apply in instances where cases of fraud have been confirmed or other irregularities/
 misrepresentation of facts has been established on the part of an EHCP under PM-JAY. This guideline
 should be read in conjunction with provision of other relevant guidelines issued by NHA such as Anti-fraud
 Guidelines, De-empanelment Guidelines, Grievance Redressal Guidelines, Whistle Blower Policy, Claims
 Adjudication and Claim Settlement Guidelines etc. and other legal recourse/provisions available to State
 for action against errant entities.
 One or more of the following actions may be taken against an errant EHCP which has been found to have
 committed any irregularity and/or illegality and/or have violated guidelines and/ or terms and conditions
 of the agreement/MoU/contract:
 A.   The ‘De-empanelment Guidelines’ has already been issued by NHA. As per relevant provision of this
      guideline and the MoU/contract signed between State and empanelled EHCP, process to be followed is
      outlined, wherein prima facie the EHCP is found to be indulging in malpractices/unethical practices.
      The key steps are as follows:
      1.   Issuance of ‘Show cause’ to errant EHCP: Based on the audit of the EHCP, if the State Health Agency/
           Insurance Company believes that there is clear evidence of EHCP indulging in malpractices/
           unethical practices or does not have adequate infrastructure/specialist manpower or has
           misrepresented facts for empanelment under the scheme, a show cause-notice shall be issued to
           the EHCP.
      2.   Suspension of EHCP: For the EHCPs which have been issued show cause notice or if the State
           Empanelment Committee (SEC) observes at any stage that it has data/ evidence that suggests
           that the EHCP is involved in any unethical practice or is not adhering to the major clauses of the
           MoU/contract with the Insurance Company or is involved in financial fraud related to treatment
           provided under the scheme, it may immediately suspend the EHCP from providing services under
           the scheme and a formal investigation shall be instituted.
      3.   De-empanelment of EHCP: If the formal investigation conducted confirms that the EHCP is indeed
           indulging in malpractices, the SEC may de-empanel the provider after following the due process
           of de-empanelment.
      4.   Recovery of amount including penalties from EHCP: Once it is confirmed that the EHCP has been
           indulging in malpractices/ misrepresentation of facts, recovery of excess amount paid to EHCP
           for fraudulent claims or illegal collection of money from beneficiaries as well as penalties levied
           depending on the severity of the offence, as described under De-empanelment Guidelines, shall
           be made from EHCP.
 a.   SHA may recover payment made against wrongful claims or penalties imposed or illegal collection of
      money from beneficiaries for treatment provided under the scheme from errant EHCPs by any of the
      following means:
      1.   Adjusting against any amount due to EHCP arising out of unpaid claims
      2.   Recourse available under MoU/contractual provisions

                                                     66
       3.   Recovery of the amount due (including penalties) as if it is a sum recoverable as an arrear of land
            revenue under provisions of the Revenue Recovery Act, 1890 and/or other relevant act(s).
  b.   Legal and Punitive Action that can be taken against errant EHCP:
       1.   Suspension of EHCP from PM-JAY scheme
       2.   De-empanelment of EHCP from PM-JAY scheme
       3.   Actions like deregistration, cancellation of license of EHCP under provisions and acts of State Govt.
            or any other relevant act of the Central Govt. such as the Clinical Establishment (Registration and
            Regulation) Act 2010 etc.
  c.   Action against Doctors/ Paramedics: The information about errant medical and paramedical
       professionals found to be indulging in malpractices or unethical practices may be provided to the
       concerned Council or Professional body requesting for cancellation/suspension of relevant license/
       registration.
  d.   Action under Criminal Law: The criminal case (FIR) may be filed against the concerned under the
       relevant provisions of the applicable law.
  e.   No review or revision against the order of recovery may be entertained by the competent authority
       unless minimum 50% of the amount ordered to be recovered is deposited by the EHCP.
  These provisions should be included in all the agreements/MoU to be entered into with all EHCPs henceforth.

Ayushman Bharat Pradhan Mantri Jan Arogya Yojana (AB PM-JAY) was launched by the Hon’ble Prime Minister
of India, Shri Narendra Modi on 23rd September 2018 in Ranchi, Jharkhand. AB PM-JAY is the largest health
assurance scheme in the world which aims at providing a health cover of Rs. 5 lakhs per family per year for
secondary and tertiary care hospitalisation.

1. Introduction to AB PM-JAY
1.1 Coverage & Scheme objective
  The scheme aims to establish a network of healthcare facilities equipped to meet the immediate
  hospitalization needs of eligible beneficiary families for both secondary and tertiary care services on a
  cashless basis, thereby safeguarding them from the financial hardship associated with medical emergencies.
  The Ayushman Bharat Pradhan Mantri Jan Arogya Yojana (AB PM-JAY) initially covered 10.74 crore eligible
  rural and urban households, identified primarily through the Socio-Economic Caste Census (SECC) 2011
  database. In rural areas, households were selected based on specified deprivation categories, while in urban
  areas, identified occupational categories were used. Additionally, families eligible under the Rashtriya
  Swasthya Bima Yojana (RSBY) but not reflected in the SECC database were also included.
  In 2022, the beneficiary base was expanded to 12 crore families, reflecting an 11.7% decadal growth
  rate. States were provided flexibility to use other databases of similar socioeconomic profiles to verify
  beneficiaries who could not be identified through SECC and related resources.
  Further expansions were introduced in March 2024, when 37.19 lakh families of Accredited Social Health
  Activists (ASHAs), field-level health functionaries, Anganwadi Workers (AWWs), and Anganwadi Helpers
  (AWHs) were added. In October 2024, coverage was extended to 4.5 crore families of senior citizens aged
  70 years and above.
  Accordingly, as of FY 2024–25, the total beneficiary base under AB PM-JAY stands at 15.14 crore families.
  The scheme is fully funded by the Government of India, with the cost of implementation shared between the
  Central and State Governments.
1.2 Objectives of AB PM-JAY Scheme
  The objective of Ayushman Bharat Pradhan Mantri Jan Arogya Yojana is to reduce catastrophic health
  expenditure, improve access to quality healthcare, reduce unmet healthcare needs, and lower out-of-pocket
  expenditure for eligible beneficiary families. Under the scheme, eligible AB PM-JAY beneficiary families are
  entitled to cashless coverage for secondary, tertiary, and day-care procedures, as applicable, through the
  network of Empanelled Health Care Providers (EHCPs).
1.3 AB PM-JAY benefits
  Eligible AB PM-JAY beneficiary families shall be provided coverage for secondary, tertiary, and day-care
  procedures (as applicable) for the treatment of diseases and medical conditions through the network of
  EHCPs. The cover under the scheme includes all expenses incurred on the following components of the
  treatment:
  a.   Medical examination, inpatient treatment and consultation.
  b.   Day care procedures.
  c.   Pre-hospitalisation up to 3 days.




                                                    115
  d.   Medicine and medical consumables.
  e.   Non-intensive and intensive care services.
  f.   Diagnostic and laboratory investigations.
  g.   Medical implantation services (where necessary).
  h.   Food services.
  i.   Complications arising during treatment.
  j.   Post-hospitalization follow-up care up to 15 days.


  The benefit cover of ₹5 lakh per family per year is provided on a family floater basis, which means it may
  be utilised by one or more members of the family. AB PM-JAY does not impose any cap on family size or age
  of members. In addition, pre-existing diseases are covered from the first day of enrolment. All treatment
  components listed above are to be provided on a cashless and paperless basis through EHCPs.
  Under the AB PM-JAY scheme, there is no age limit or restriction on family size, ensuring coverage for
  all members of eligible families regardless of age, gender, or pre-existing conditions. Beneficiaries are not
  required to undergo any pre-insurance health check-up or medical examination for eligibility determination,
  and all pre-existing illnesses are covered. This universal approach guarantees comprehensive protection
  without discrimination. In October 2024, the scheme was further expanded to include all citizens aged 70
  years and above, irrespective of income, thereby strengthening its inclusivity and reach.


2. Health Benefit Packages (HBPs)
2.1 Introduction
  Health Benefit Packages (HBPs) under AB PM-JAY are a defined set of covered healthcare services, medical
  and surgical treatments, and corresponding package rates made available to eligible beneficiaries on a
  cashless basis through EHCPs, subject to the provisions of the scheme. HBPs include inpatient hospitalisation
  and day-care treatments, along with associated diagnostics, medicines, implants, procedures, pre- and post-
  hospitalisation care, follow-up care, and other treatment-related services as specified in this guideline and
  the HBP Package Master.
  HBPs may be broadly classified into two categories: medical and surgical. Medical HBPs include both fixed-
  cost packages and per-day medical management packages. Fixed-cost medical packages cover specialties
  such as Medical Oncology, Radiation Oncology, and Interventional Radiology, Cardiology while per-day
  medical management packages apply to specialties like General Medicine, Paediatrics medical management,
  etc. In contrast, all surgical HBPs are designed as fixed-cost packages, ensuring standardized coverage
  across surgical procedures.
2.2 Objective
  The objective of HBPs under AB PM-JAY is to standardise covered entitlements and package-based payments
  for eligible treatments, so as to improve access to quality healthcare services, reduce out-of-pocket and
  catastrophic health expenditure, and promote transparency, cost control, and uniformity in service delivery
  across EHCPs.
2.3 Journey of HBPs

  going rationalisation process (Detailed in section 2.4 below). These versions are HBP 1.0, 2.0, 2.1, 2.2, and
  2022. Figure 1 below outlines the evolution of HBPs and may be updated as and when new HBP versions
  are introduced. HBPs are continuously evolving, not static, and that updates are part of an ongoing process.

2.4   Need and Process of Rationalisation
  The rationalisation of HBPs is an important and ongoing process undertaken to expand the range of
  treatments covered under the scheme, thereby making the packages more comprehensive. HBPs are
  rationalised across its various versions to address the following issues on an ongoing basis, if:
      a.      rates for several packages are inadequate to cover the cost of procedures.
      b.      duplication of packages is observed both within individual specialties and across specialties.
      c.      nomenclature and terminology of packages lack consistency.
      d.      some procedures overlap with services already covered under ongoing national health programmes.
      e.      new treatments are to be added based on the future requirements.
      f.      many procedures are being booked under unspecified packages in the absence of specific treatment
              packages (Detailed in section 3.11 below).
      g.      analysis suggest some state specific packages to be added into the national HBP package master.
  The rationalisation process helps ensure that beneficiaries of AB PM-JAY are able to access necessary
  healthcare services without incurring significant out-of-pocket expenditure. The rationalisation also aims
  at enhancing the quality, relevance, and effectiveness of the package services by revising package rates,
  correcting nomenclature, and aligning the packages with prevailing clinical practices and global standards.
  The NHA undertakes these rationalisation activities through consultations with members of the Medical
  Advisory Cell at the national level, with a view to addressing the concerns of all stakeholders. This exercise
  also contributes to making HBPs more economically viable and comprehensive by incorporating additional
  treatments and improving the overall structure of the package master. Specialty-wise meetings are
  conducted with subject matter experts to seek expert inputs and recommendations. Based on the expert
  suggestions and technical guidance, HBPs under AB PM-JAY are designed and revised. These consultations
  may be conducted either in person or virtually, as & when required by NHA.
  Using the above explained process, the following activities are undertaken on a regular basis:
      a.      Analysis of unspecified surgical packages booked across the country.
      b.      Gather and review feedback from Medical Advisory Cell.
      c.      Examination of stakeholder/States requests for inclusion of additional treatments or packages.
      d.      Analysis of utilisation patterns under existing packages.
      e.      Analysis of state-specific packages across the country.
      f.      Request received from HeFTA unit.

      d.      Bed charges

3. Salient Features of National Health Benefit Packages
The various salient features of National HBPs are explained below:
3.1 Packages and Procedures
   Packages and procedures were introduced as separate entities from HBP 2.0 onwards. A package is split
   into procedures to specify the approach, treatment modality, etiology, complication, or other relevant
   variation within that package. Recording procedures instead of only packages allows more detailed analysis
   of service utilisation under the scheme.
   For example, the package “Lap/Open Cholecystectomy with/without Exploration of CBD” is divided into the
   following four independent and specific procedures:
      a.   Lap cholecystectomy with exploration of CBD
      b.   Lap cholecystectomy without exploration of CBD
      c.   Open cholecystectomy with exploration of CBD
      d.   Open cholecystectomy without exploration of CBD
   Significance for users: The list of packages serves as a reference field for the end user while selecting the
   procedure to be performed. However, the final selection is always made at the procedure level. While
   searching for a procedure, the TMS searches across both package names and procedure names to present
   the closest match, thereby making selection easier and reducing ambiguity.
3.2 Stratification
   Stratification was introduced to further classify procedures based on factors such as type of anaesthesia,
   bed category at admission, surgical technique etc. This helps capture variations within the same procedure
   and allows differential pricing to better reflect the nature and complexity of the service provided.
   For example, the procedure “Incision and Drainage of Abscess” is split into two stratified options with a
   price difference of Rs 5,000:
      a.   Incision and Drainage of Abscess under Local Anaesthesia
      b.   Incision and Drainage of Abscess under General Anaesthesia
   Significance for users: While total price calculation continues to happen at the back end of the TMS, the
   end user may need to enter certain procedure-specific details that affect stratification. For example, after


                                                       119
  selecting the procedure, the TMS may ask for details such as the type of anaesthesia anticipated. In some
  cases, the system may automatically identify relevant criteria from patient details, such as whether the
  procedure should be treated as paediatric or adult. These system-based assumptions may be overridden by
  the treating physician based on clinical judgement. However, such overrides may trigger a medical audit,
  and it is therefore advisable to maintain adequate supporting documentation to justify such cases.
3.2.1 Stratification in Medical Management Packages (Per day)
  All medical management packages across HBP versions are stratified based on the bed category at the time
  of patient admission. Accordingly, the relevant bed category must be selected while choosing the diagnosis.
  The cost of a medical management package is calculated on a per-day basis, depending on the selected bed
  category. The total package amount is determined by multiplying the applicable per-day rate by the number
  of bed days for the patient.
  The applicable per-day rates for different bed categories vary according to the relevant HBP version. For
  example, the per-day bed rates under the HBP 2022 National Package Master are as follows:
     a.   ₹ 2100 for Routine Ward.
     b.   ₹ 3,300 for High Dependency unit (HDU).
     c.   ₹ 8,500 for Intensive Care Unit (ICU) without ventilator support.
     d.   ₹ 9000 for Intensive Care Unit with ventilator support.
  In case of auto-approved cases, the pre-authorization is automatically approved for the first day of admission
  in per-day medical management packages. If a per-day medical case requires hospitalisation beyond the
  auto-approved period, it will be managed as per the details given in section 3.9 below.
  Significance for users: This feature does not result in any major change at the user end. The TMS continues
  to calculate the final package amount of the medical management package based on the selected patient
  bed category and the patient’s length of stay.
3.3 Cross Specialty Packages
  Cross speciality packages are relevant to more than one specialty. Such packages are listed under all relevant
  specialties without duplicating the unique package code and maintaining a uniform price for the same
  procedure across the relevant specialties. This helps in reducing the duplication, maintaining uniformity,
  and minimizing the risk of upcoding by billing the same package under a different specialty.
  For example, the package of ‘Temporary pacemaker implantation’ is a cross-specialty package between
  Cardiology and Cardiothoracic and Vascular Surgery (CTVS) and therefore appears under both specialities.
  Significance for users: This approach helps in removing the duplication of packages across specialties,
  avoids price differential for the same package across specialties, and reduce ambiguities in package names.
  The users are required to search through a shorter list of packages to seek out the desired procedure. The
  cross-specialty feature also allows certain packages to be available across specialties without unnecessarily
  adding on to the number of packages. For instance, the procedure of “Tracheostomy/Tracheotomy” is now
  available to multiple specialties at a fixed rate irrespective of the specialty under which it is booked.
3.4 Add-on Packages
  Add-on packages are those that may be performed along with a primary package. These are mapped to
  specific primary packages and can be booked in the TMS along with the primary package, without altering
  the identity or base price of the primary package. No deduction shall be applied to the payment of such add-
  on packages. For example, Negative Pressure Wound Therapy (NPWT) under Plastic Surgery.
3.4.1 Booking of high-end diagnostics and investigation add-on packages
  Similarly, in per-day medical management packages, certain high-end diagnostics and investigations may
  be allowed as add-ons with mapped primary packages. These include:


                                                     120
  a.   High-end radiological diagnostics such as CT, MRI, and other imaging, including nuclear imaging,
       relevant to the illness; and
  b.   High-end histopathology (biopsies) and advanced serology investigations.
These add-ons are not allowed as standalone packages. These are payable only:
  a.   along with the mapped primary medical management package,
  b.   subject to pre-authorisation, and
  c.   up to the applicable cap, such as Rs. 5,000/- per family per annum on actuals within the overall sum
       insured, wherever specified in the relevant HBP version.
  In general, per-day medical management packages may allow such add-on diagnostics where specified,
  whereas surgical packages are usually all-inclusive and do not permit addition of separate diagnostic
  packages. However, under HBP 2022 version, certain high-end drugs, diagnostics, and procedures have
  been specifically included as add-ons with selected primary medical and surgical packages (Refer to HBP
  2022 National Package Master for list of such add-on packages).
  Significance for users: There is no significant change at the user end on account of various HBP versions.
  The TMS will continue to calculate the final price of the package/packages at the back end, taking into
  account the applicable add-on rules and mapped package conditions.
3.5 Standalone Packages
  Certain procedures that may not be claimed as enhancements or clubbed to other existing/unspecified
  surgical packages are termed as Standalone packages. This has been done primarily to reduce the incidence
  of abuse of the packages yet keeping them available for use.
  For example, the package of ‘Laparoscopic Adhesiolysis’ under Obstetrics and Gynaecology (OBG) is
  classified as a standalone package and cannot be booked in combination with any other package.
3.6 Follow up Packages
  Follow-up packages have been introduced from HBP 2.0 onwards for selected procedures. These packages
  enable the EHCP to maintain a continuum of care for the patient beyond the 15-day period covered under
  the original AB PM-JAY package. They also allow the patient to seek follow up care, when required, from a
  centre other than the one where the original procedure was performed.
  For example, a follow up procedure for ‘Stoma Management, Follow up of Colostomy’ has been introduced
  in the HBP 2.0 version and onwards for patients requiring such care.
  Significance for the user: A follow up procedure may be booked by the treating EHCP with clear supporting
  documentation establishing the need for such care. This documentation should include proof of the original
  procedure performed, along with details of the activities to be undertaken as part of the follow up care of
  the patient. Since such packages may be prone to misuse, their utilisation is closely monitored by NHA to
  assess their continued relevance and inclusion in future revisions of the HBP.
3.7 Abuse Prone Packages
  Certain packages are abuse prone and require closer monitoring. Such packages have been flagged in the
  TMS, and a conscious attempt has been made to keep the price of abuse prone packages at the minimum
  level to minimise incentives for abuse. The booking of these packages may also generate alerts for the
  National Anti-Fraud Unit (NAFU) at the NHA and may trigger further scrutiny or investigation.
  In addition, anti-fraud advisories are issued from time to time to guide states on measures for preventing,
  detecting, and addressing malpractices related to fraud- and abuse-prone packages. Fraud control
  mechanisms, including system-based alerts, triggers and machine learning algorithms, have also been
  deployed to monitor package utilization and flag suspicious cases for medical audit and investigation. The
  detailed process for medical audit of such suspect cases is provided in the Field Investigation and Medical
  Audit Manual.

3.9 Auto Approval Packages
  Certain packages are emergency in nature and therefore do not require approval from the Pre-authorisation
  Processing Doctor (PPD). In such cases, the package is automatically approved within the TMS, enabling the
  hospital to proceed with treatment without delay. Such packages are termed auto-approved packages, and
  the default turnaround time of six hours applicable for PPD approval does not apply to them. E.g. “Congenital
  Atresia & Stenosis of Small Intestine.” Under the CTVS speciality.
  Further, in the case of per-day medical management packages, the first day of pre-authorisation is
  automatically approved by the system. However, any subsequent enhancement request for extended
  hospitalisation is routed to the PPD for review and approval. In such cases, the Medical Coordinator
  (MEDCO) may submit enhancement requests for a maximum of two additional days for private EHCPs and
  five additional days for public EHCPs. For example, ‘Hydrocephalus’ under the General Medicine specialty..
  As per O.M. No. S-12015/08/2019-NHA(HNW&QA) Part22 dated 18th August 2025 (Annexure 3), 97
  procedures to continue under auto-approval, 429 removed from auto-approval status (non-emergency
  in nature), and 120 per-day medical management procedures will retain day-1 as auto-approval with
  subsequent days approval with enhancement of additional number of days as explained above.
3.10 Booking Multiple Packages with Different Combinations
3.10.1 Surgical-Surgical combination
  Under AB PM-JAY, procedures that are flagged as surgical packages may be clubbed together in the TMS. In
  cases where a beneficiary undergoes multiple surgical procedures during a single OT session, the procedure
  with the highest package rate shall be considered the primary package and reimbursed at 100% of the
  package rate. The second surgical procedure shall be reimbursed at 50% of the package rate, while the third
  and any subsequent surgical procedures shall be reimbursed at 25% of the respective package rates.
3.10.2 Surgical-Other than Per day medical combination
  Surgical and other than-per-day medical packages may be clubbed under a single pre-authorisation. No
  deduction shall apply, and the full package rate for each package shall be reimbursed.
3.10.3 Surgical-Per day medical combination
  Surgical and per-day medical management packages shall not be allowed under the same pre-authorisation.
  In exceptional circumstances, the hospital may raise a specific request, which shall be decided by the SHA
  with the support of the concerned medical specialist
3.10.4 Medical-Medical combination (Other than per day medical packages)
  When two medical packages are clubbed, no deduction shall be applied, and the full package rate for each
  package shall be reimbursed. E.g. medical packages from specialties such as Medical Oncology and Radiation
  Oncology, Cardiology etc.
3.10.5 Medical-Medical combination (Per Day Medical Management Packages)
  Per-day medical management packages under Health Benefit Packages are stratified across four categories:
  Routine Ward, High Dependency Unit (HDU), Intensive Care Unit (ICU) without ventilator, and ICU with
  ventilator, as described in Section 3.2.1 above. These per-day medical management packages shall not be
  clubbed together under the same pre-authorisation.

3.11 Unspecified Surgical Packages
  To ensure that AB PM-JAY beneficiaries are not denied necessary care, a provision for Unspecified Surgical
  Packages (USPs) has been made for surgical treatments/procedures that are not available in the applicable
  package master. Further, to expedite the approval process of USP and provide more flexibility to states/UTs,
  the Governing Board of NHA decided to delegate the authority to approve requests for booking USP up to
  Rs. 5 lakh to respective SHA (O.M. No. S-12012/183/2022-NHA(Pt.1), dated 01 February 2022 (Annexure
  4) and as per O.M. No. S-12018/353/2023-NHA, dated 13th February 2026 (Annexure 5), the authority of
  USP approval has been given to CEO of the respective SHA.
3.11.1 Key Aspects of USPs
  An Unspecified Surgical Package (USP) may be considered only where a required surgical treatment or
  procedure is not available in the applicable HBP master and where denial of care would otherwise result.
  Approval of a USP up to ₹5 lakh is delegated to the SHA, subject to examination by the SHA Medical Committee
  and approval by the CEO, SHA. USPs should be used only in exceptional or life-saving circumstances and
  should not be used for medical management cases, exclusion criteria, government-reserved packages
  booked by private hospitals, or individual drugs and diagnostics. Some of the key aspects of USPs are given
  in detail below:
  a.   Every Unspecified Surgical Package (USP) request shall be examined by the SHA Medical Committee
       before being placed to the CEO, SHA. The request, along with the Committee’s opinion, shall then
       be submitted to the CEO, SHA for approval. The CEO, SHA has the authority to approve unspecified
       surgical packages up to ₹5 lakhs. SHAs shall maintain a record of the Medical Committee’s opinion and
       the reasons for approval or rejection recorded by the CEO, SHA, for audit purposes.
  b.   approved USPs with paid claims with NHA and the SHA Governing Board for information and review.
       NHA may review the list of approved USPs shared by SHAs for possible inclusion under the National or
       State Package Master.
  c.   To book packages under unspecified category, the package should not be listed under the respective
       HBP master.
  d.   Benefits under the USP shall be available to the beneficiary on a cashless and paperless basis at any
       Empanelled Health Care Provider (EHCP), subject to the available balance in the beneficiary’s AB PM-
       JAY wallet/sum insured.
  e.   Compulsory pre-authorization is in-built while selecting USP code for blocking treatments and cannot
       be raised under multiple package selection.
  f.   USP is not applicable for medical packages. (E.g., General Medicine, Paediatric Medical Management,
       Interventional Radiology, Cardiology, Medical Oncology, Radiation Oncology etc.).
  g.   Government reserved packages cannot be availed by private hospitals under USP code. PPD/Claims
       Processing Doctor (CPD) may reject such claims on these grounds. In addition, SHA may circulate
       respective government reserved packages to all empanelled hospitals. Further, states need to establish
       suitable mechanisms to refer such cases to the public system as a means to avoid denial of care.
  h.   USP cannot be booked for removal of implants that were inserted under the same policy. However, in
       cases where implant removal is not covered under any other package, prior approval may be obtained
       from the State Health Agency or the National Health Authority.
  i.   In portability cases, the approval team of the patient’s home State may reject a request where
       a government-reserved package of the patient State has been selected by a private hospital in the


                                                    123
       treating State, unless the case is considered on emergency grounds
  j.   Aesthetic treatments of any nature cannot be availed as USP or as such under any other listed codes
       under AB PM-JAY. Only medically necessary with functional purpose/indications can be covered. The
       procedure should result in improving/restoring bodily function or to correct significant deformity
       resulting from accidental injury, trauma or to address congenital anomalies that have resulted in
       significant functional impairment.
  k.   Individual drugs or diagnostics cannot be availed under USP code. Only listed drugs and diagnostics
       with fixed price schedules, listed under the drop down of respective specialties, are included for
       blocking treatments.
  l.   None of the treatments that fall under the exclusion (Annexure 6) under AB PM-JAY can be availed
       viz. individual diagnostics for evaluation, out-patient care, drug rehabilitation, cosmetic or aesthetic
       treatments, vaccination, hormone replacement therapy for sex change or any treatment related to sex
       change, any dental treatment or surgery which is corrective, cosmetic or of aesthetic procedure, filling
       of cavity, root canal including wear and tear, etc. unless arising from disease or injury and which need
       hospitalisation for treatment etc. However, for life threatening cases e.g. of suicide attempt or accident
       due to excess consumption of alcohol, treatment shall be provided by the hospital till the patient’s
       condition stabilises.
  m.   For deciding the approval amount, the PPD may consider the rate of the closest matching surgery
       available under listed AB PM-JAY packages. The amount approved by the PPD shall be treated as final,
       shall be communicated to the hospital, and shall not be subject to subsequent deduction or partial
       approval by the CPD.
  n.   For any state/UTs to utilise the USP, it is to be ensured that the same is approved only in (a) exceptional
       circumstances and/or (b) for life saving conditions.
       i. Exceptional circumstances may include:
          • Rare disease conditions or rare surgeries.
          • Procedure available under HBP in a different specialty but not available in the treating specialty.
          • Procedure available under HBP in a specialty for which the hospital is not empanelled.
          • Other conditions treatments which are not excluded under AB PM-JAY but not listed in HBP.
       ii. Life-saving conditions may include:
          • Emergencies or life-threatening conditions

5 Package Rates
Under AB PM-JAY, two types of package rates have been defined across all versions: fixed-rate packages, which
are generally applicable to surgical procedures, and per-day packages, which are typically used for per-day
medical management.
The Insurance Company (IC)/trust shall reimburse claims of the EHCP under AB PM-JAY based on Package
Rates determined as follows:
     • Final Procedure Price = Procedure price + Stratification rate (if any) + Incentive (if applicable) +
       Implant(s) rate (if any).
The components of the final procedure price are explained in the sections below.
5.1 Procedure Price
The package rate for any medical treatment or surgical procedure requiring hospitalisation or day-care
treatment, as applicable, is specified in the Health Benefit Package list. Such package rate shall remain
applicable for the duration of the policy cover period. In case of HBP 2022 the concept of differential pricing
was introduced in the national health benefit package master.
For the purpose of reimbursement of hospitalisation or day care treatment expenses, the package rate shall
be treated as a comprehensive rate and shall include all costs associated with the treatment, including, but not
limited to, the treatments covered in section 2.5 above.
HBP 2022 introduced the concept of differential pricing in the National Health Benefit Package Master. Under
this approach, package rates are adjusted based on the type of city and whether the hospital is a teaching
hospital. The differential price weights were derived from cost function analysis using data from the Cost
of Health Services in India (CHSI) study. These weights were used to determine the relative reimbursement
levels for hospitals located in Tier 2 and Tier 3 cities compared with the reference price for Tier 1 cities.
Similarly, an additional factor was derived to account for the higher costs incurred by teaching hospitals. Using
these factors, the base price was adjusted to arrive at the final procedure price for Tier 1, Tier 2, and Tier 3
cities across secondary and tertiary care procedures, separately for medical and surgical packages. The price
weights for differential pricing are provided in table 2 below.

                       Table 2: Price Weights as per Differential Pricing under HBP 2022

5.2 Stratification Rate
In HBPs, certain procedures have been identified where different treatment modalities exist for the same
or similar procedure, such as differences in type of anaesthesia, surgical approach, laterality, or aetiology.


                                                      137
Although these differences may appear minor, they may have significant cost implications. Such procedures
have therefore been classified as stratified procedures, with an additional stratification layer introduced to
account for this variation in effort and cost.

5.4 Splitting Cost of Implants
The cost of implants has been considered separately while calculating the package rate in the system. This
allows the system to vary the procedure cost depending on the type and number of implants used during the
procedure.
For example, package price of a ‘Balloon Pulmonary Valvotomy’ in Cardiology would vary depending on the
use of a paediatric/adult cardiac balloon. Price of plates/screws in orthopaedics would also be calculated at
the back end based on the number of implants used.
Significance for the user: This feature does not materially change the process at the user end across HBP
versions. However, it provides important long-term advantages by enabling variation in implant cost to be
reflected in the final package calculation. It also allows pricing to vary according to the number and type of
implants used and adds flexibility for inclusion of new implant categories as the scheme evolves. The TMS will
continue to calculate the final package price at the back end in accordance with these parameters.
5.5 Step payment of staged procedures
Packages that require multiple stages for complete administration of therapy, with long gaps in between have
been split into stages with independent payment of each stage. This is expected to facilitate payment to a
treating hospital, if the patient decides to continue/follow up on his/her treatment at a different facility.
For example, ‘Hypospadias repair - Two or more stages’ in Urology will now be paid on each, or either of the
following stages:
     a. First stage
     b. Intermediate stage
     c. Final stage
Significance for the user: For all staged procedures, the TMS may require the treating physician to specify the
stage of surgery he/she is anticipating performing at the time of booking of the package.
5.6 Co-Payment
AB PM-JAY is a cashless scheme, and no beneficiary should ordinarily be required to pay for treatment at
any EHCP. However, co-payment may be permitted in certain limited situations, subject to applicable policy
provisions and availability of the feature in the TMS for the respective state.
Co-payment may be allowed where:
     1. the cost of the package exceeds the beneficiary’s available wallet balance under AB PM-JAY; or
     2. the package cost itself is higher than the maximum wallet limit of Rs. 5 lakh, such as in the case of
        certain high-cost procedures.
Significance for the user: For all staged procedures, the TMS may require the treating physician to specify the
stage of surgery he/she is anticipating performing at the time of booking of the package.
This provision enables the beneficiary to utilise the available balance in the AB PM-JAY wallet and arrange the
remaining amount through permitted additional funding sources, instead of being denied access to treatment
due to insufficiency of funds.
Accordingly, where the beneficiary’s wallet balance is insufficient for a listed AB PM-JAY package, the beneficiary
may avail treatment by using the AB PM-JAY wallet along with additional funding from one or more of the
following sources:
      a. self-contribution by the beneficiary;
      b. top-up support through another Central or State Government scheme, such as Rashtriya Arogya Nidhi
         (RAN), Health Minister’s Discretionary Grant (HMDG), or any other eligible scheme. Several states/
         UTs have implemented schemes with provision of higher funding for rare/life threatening diseases;
         or

      c. self-contribution in cases where the package cost exceeds the overall wallet limit of Rs. 5 lakh e.g., lungs

6. Payment in Special Cases during Hospital Admission

Once a patient is admitted under AB PM-JAY in an empanelled hospital, in normal course the patient will be
discharged by the hospital after completing the treatment. Hence in majority of cases, the payment to the
hospital will be done based on the package booked and rates prescribed for that package.
However, in some cases, this may not happen due to various reasons e.g. patient may leave against medical
advice, patient may die within the hospital or patient may need to be referred to another hospital.
In these special cases clarity needs to be provided to both hospitals and the payers (State Health Agency/IC)
regarding payments to the hospitals. These guidelines provide details of payments to be done in these special
cases.
The basic principles to be followed in implementation of these guidelines are as follows:
   •    Hospitals shall be eligible for partial payment only if they notify the respective SHA/ISA/IC through
        the IT platform of any deviation from the normal course of treatment as soon as possible and within
        24 hours of the event. For public hospitals, this reporting time limit may be relaxed up to 72 hours.
   •    Additionally, in each of these cases payment will be done only after a successful audit by the SHA/IC.
   •    The audit process shall be completed by the SHA/IC within 15 days of receiving the information from
        the hospital.
   •    It is expected that these deviations would not amount to more than 5% in a particular hospital.
6.1 Patient Leave/Discharge Against Medical Advice (LAMA/DAMA)
Leave Against Medical Advice (LAMA) and Discharge Against Medical Advice (DAMA) are related but not
identical terms and are often used interchangeably in practice. In general, both refer to a situation in which a
patient leaves the hospital before the treating physician recommends discharge. LAMA usually refers to the
patient leaving the hospital on their own request (or at the request of the family/attendant), while DAMA
refers to the discharge being formally documented as being against medical advice.

        The hospital (empanelled) that receives the referred patient will be eligible for 100% of the package
        rate of the surgery booked by the hospital.
        Referral after PAC but before surgery in case surgery is abandoned & patient is transferred - In
        this case, the hospital that has referred the patient will be paid 15% of the package amount for the
        surgical package booked by the hospital. The hospital that has received the referred patient will be
        provided 85% of the package rate of the surgery selected in the hospital. The receiving hospital will
        need to take pre- authorization before booking the package.

    c. Medical cases
        Payment for 100% of the daily package rate for the full number of days for which the patient was
        admitted will be paid to the referring hospital as per the category of ward. Required documentation
        (clinical notes) for each full day will need to be submitted for payment to be considered. The hospital

        (if a surgical package is booked), or in line with the existing medical packages according to Length of
        Stay (LOS) and bed category of the patient (if a medical package is booked), depending on the patient.

7 Grievance Redressal
Empanelled hospitals, acting on behalf of beneficiaries or in respect of issues faced by the hospitals, may
register grievances through the Central Grievance Redressal Management System (CGRMS) portal (http://
cgrms.nha.gov.in) in connection with the delivery of healthcare services under the scheme. In cases where a
hospital contacts the Hospital Helpline at 14413 and requests that a grievance be raised on its behalf, the call
centre team is authorized to register the grievance on behalf of the hospital.
Grievances classified as SOS (emergency cases) shall be resolved within a turnaround time (TAT) of 5 hours,
whereas non-emergency (Non-SOS) cases shall be resolved within 15 days.
For detailed grievance redressal procedures, please refer to the “Beneficiary Engagement Guidelines.

8.2 Raising a Pre-Auth in context to HBP Guidelines
8.2.1 Key Considerations
    a. Packages require pre-authorisation from the IC/trust before treatment can be administered. If any
       required inpatient treatment is not available in the defined package master list, the hospital may
       provide such treatment to the beneficiary only after obtaining approval from the IC/trust, and the
       case shall be processed as an unspecified package. In both situations, the operator may initiate a pre-
       authorisation request to the IC/trust through the web application.
    b. The hospital operator will send all documents required for pre-authorization to the IC/trust using the
       Centralized TMS/States transaction management application.
    c. The documents needed may vary from package to package and hence a master list of all documents
       required for all packages is available within the system.
    d. The IC/trust shall approve or reject the request within six hours. If no decision is taken within this
       period, the request shall be deemed approved by default.
    e. In case of an emergency or delay in getting the response for pre-authorization request due to technical
       issues, provision will be there to get the pre-authorization code over the phone from IC/Trust or
       the call centre setup by IC/Trust. The documents required for the processing, may be sent using the
       transaction system within stipulated time.
    f. In case of emergency, IC/trust will provide the pre-authorization code generated through the
       algorithm/utility provided by MoHFW/NHA-NIC.
    g. Pre-authorization code provided by the IC/Trust will be entered by the operator and will be verified
       by the system.
    h. If pre-authorization request is rejected, insurance company/ Trust will provide the reasons for
       rejection. Rejection details will be captured and stored in the transaction database.
    i. If the beneficiary or the hospital are not satisfied by the rejection reason, they can appeal through
       grievance system or hospital can resubmit the pre-auth after addressing the rejection reasons.

8.2.3 Approving a pre-auth
In addition to the activities listed in Claim Adjudication Manual 2026 on account of the HBP 2.0/ HBP2.1/
HBP 2.2/HBP 2022, the PPD at the TPA/ISA would additionally need to check the following before approving
a pre-auth.
    a. Conformity of the selected procedure with the patient’s clinical condition - Since the HBP 2.0/ HBP
       2.1/ HBP 2.2/HBP 2022 are significantly more granular than the previous version (HBP 1.0), the
       detail of the procedure selected will need to be validated by the patient condition. Packages such as
       congenital heart diseases, peripheral arterial surgeries, etc. have been split into multiple procedures
       that fall within the same package, but have significant differences in terms of management, skill and
       therefore, cost. This needs to be looked into and considered closely before approving a pre-auth for the
       procedure.
    b. Selected implant in conformity with the age, gender, and condition of the patient - Certain implants in
       the HBP 1.0/ HBP 2.0/ HBP 2.1/ HBP 2.2/HBP 2022 are distinguished based on the age of the patient.
       Paediatric implants/devices are recommended for patients less than or equal to 14 years of age, while
       adult devices are to be used beyond that age. Once more, keeping the cost implication in mind, it will
       need to be verified whether the implant/device is in conformation with the age of the patient. Similarly,

                                                     149
        some implants are gender specific, and some consumables are required only in select patients. Number
        of screws, plates and other implants are also variable in HBP 2.0/ HBP 2.1/ HBP 2.2/HBP 2022 and
        their anticipated numbers for a surgery will need to be verified before approving a patient pre-auth.
     c. Selected stratification in lines with the age, gender, and condition of the patient - Every stratification
        of the procedure selected is backed up with a cost implication that needs to be closely considered at
        the time of approving a pre-authorization. Performing a procedure under LA/GA, Unilateral/Bilateral
        are some examples of stratifications that will need to be closely looked into, and justified, owing to the
        price differential involved.
     d. Non-Surgical procedures - Medical care packages have been introduced in certain specialties to allow
        for continuity of care for the patient. Monitoring the justification for booking such procedures will
        therefore constitute an additional responsibility of the PPD.
     e. Follow up procedures - In HBP 2.0,2.1,2.2, and 2022 follow up procedures can be blocked for certain
        packages but will need to be supported by documentary evidence that the parent procedure has been
        performed, even if the parent procedure was performed outside hospitals empanelled under AB PM-
        JAY. Required documents list is needed.
     f. Diagnostic procedures - Certain diagnostic procedures have been included under AB PM-JAY based on
        user demand, and on the suggestions of the Medical Advisory Cell expert committees. Such procedures
        have been highlighted in HBP 2.0, HBP 2.1, HBP 2.2, and HBP 2022 are abuse prone procedures and will
        be closely monitored by the National & State Fraud monitoring teams. The PPD is advised to maintain
        a close vigil on blocking of these packages.


9 HBP Customisation for States
Packages and procedures under HBPs are developed in concurrence with panel of specialist committees
and have been approved by the Medical Advisory Cell Committee and the Governing Board of NHA. These
packages are intended to address most conditions contributing to the morbidity and mortality profile of the
population. However, States/UTs may have specific requirements based on local epidemiology, demographics,
infrastructure and implementation challenges. Accordingly, State/UTs may customise the national packages
and include new state-specific packages that are not available in the National HBP master
While the sanctity of the National Health Benefit Package master is expected to remain unchanged, States/UTs
may request certain modifications as outlined below.
9.1 Modification in prices
As per O.M. no. S-12012/183/2022-NHA (Pt.1), dated 01 February 2022 (Annexure 15), the Governing Board
of NHA has approved the proposal to give flexibility to States/UTs in deciding on design and cost of health
benefit packages under AB PM-JAY. This flexibility essentially means that till the time packages are not in
AB PM-JAY exclusion category state has the flexibility to modify the packages. NHA shall share the financial
burden as per mutual arrangement, subject to adherence to ceiling limits defined under the scheme.
The above flexibility will be subject to fulfilment of following conditions:
     a. All the packages under the National package master must be made available by the States/UTs to
        AB PM-JAY beneficiaries. Packages in the national package master, which cannot not be mapped to
        the State package master, should be included in the state package master and provided at National
        Package rates fixed by NHA or rates as decided by the State/UT.
     b. The expense on State specific packages which cannot be mapped to National Package master shall be
        shared by NHA, subject to adherence to ceiling and package exclusion guidelines as stated above.
     c. States/UTs should use the nomenclature of procedures as per the International Classification of
        Diseases (ICD 11) and International Classification of Health Interventions (ICHI) to the extent possible.

                                                       150
     d. Specialty wise medical committees must be constituted by the SHA to formulate/revise or customize
        the national package master for implementing state specific package master.
     e. Once finalised, the state package master shall be approved by Addl. Chief Secretary/Principal Secretary
        (Health) of the State/UT and ratified by the State Governing Board/Executive Board/Highest authority
        in the SHA.
     f. The approved state package master must be shared with NHA for flagging State specific packages in
        the NHA’s IT system. Any changes in the state package master must be shared on real time basis for this
        purpose.
     g. States/UTs may increase package prices by up to 10% over the NHA-configured package rates. However,
        there shall be no limit on reduction of the calibrated package rates. The additional financial burden for
        package priced beyond 110% of the NHA recommended price would be borne exclusively by the State/
        UT with no additional contribution from NHA.
9.2 Modification in public reservation
Considering the significant variance in public infrastructure among states, a provision is available to the states
to recommend, or modify the package selection that has been recommended by the NHA for public reservation
within the following considerations:
     i. Certain mental health procedures are recommended to be reserved for public hospitals considering
        the potential of fraud and abuse.
     ii. Packages related to obstetrics and deliveries should be considered to be kept public reserved
         considering the nature of the services.
     iii. Hysterectomy and related procedures feature are among the most abused procedures under AB PM-
          JAY and are therefore advised to be public reserved.
     iv. Any other procedures suggested through NAFU advisories, or as deemed fit by the states may be
         requested to be public reserved within the state specific package master.
In case the state requires any of the above to be de-reserved, a proposal with adequate justification may be
submitted by the SHA to NHA for consideration.
9.3 Inclusion of State-Specific Packages
Depending upon the incidence and prevalence of disease in the State/UT, there may be a need to include new
packages or procedures not covered under HBP 2.0, HBP 2.1, HBP 2.2, or HBP 2022.
Any State-specific package proposed for inclusion shall meet the following criteria:
     a. It should not fall within the exclusion criteria of AB PM-JAY.
     b. It should not duplicate an existing package.
     c. Its rate should be aligned with similar packages available in the national package master or in other
        schemes such as CGHS.
9.3.1 nclusion Process
     a. Process to be followed at the State
     i. The SHA would propose any packages or procedures to be added to the state specific packages based
        on the specific needs of the state.
        The proposed list, duly approved by the competent authority of the SHA, shall be forwarded to the
        HPQA Division of NHA through the NHA-nominated State Coordinator.
        Any State-specific package proposed for inclusion shall meet the following criteria:
     b. Process to be followed at NHA


                                                       151
    i. NHA shall examine the proposal and assess each package/procedure and its rate on a case-by-case
       basis for feasibility of inclusion in the State and/or national package master.
    ii. In case of any difference of opinion between NHA and the SHA, the proposal shall be referred back to
        the State/UT for revision, clarification, or further justification. Once approved by the SHA, the final
        proposal shall be formally communicated to NHA.


10 Standard Treatment Guidelines
Standard Treatment Guidelines (STGs) covering all AB PM-JAY procedures, have been developed and
integrated in the NHA Transaction Management System (TMS). The integration includes rationalization of
the mandatory documents for the respective packages and addition of few questions for the MEDCO (at the
hospital level) and the pre-authorisation and claims processing team (at the PPD and CPD level).
The objective of the STGs is to support PPDs, CPDs, TPAs, insurance companies, and SHAs in processing pre-
authorisations and claims. The STGs specify the mandatory documentation required for each procedure and
the key clinical information to be reviewed in those documents. They also support fraud and abuse control,
promote appropriate and quality care, improve uniformity in documentation across EHCPs, and serve as a
practical guidance tool for treating doctors, MEDCOs, and medical auditors.
These guidance documents are based on standard treatment workflows and treatment guidelines developed
by DHR-ICMR, the National Cancer Grid led by Tata Memorial Hospital, MoHFW, published literature, State
guidelines, and other globally accepted treatment protocols. Oncology packages mapped with available
National Cancer Grid Guidelines have been adapted and customised for HBPs under AB PM-JAY following a
rigorous process of review at NHA and by eminent clinical experts in the field.
The anticipated benefits of PMJAY STGs include:
    • Standardise the documentation submitted by EHCPs.
    • Reduce the number of queries and repeat transactions.
    • Reduce unnecessary delay in processing pre-authorisations and claims, thereby supporting timely
      payment to hospitals.
    • Improve accountability across all levels, including MEDCO, PPD, and CPD.
    • Improve quality of care by discouraging unnecessary treatment and promoting appropriateness of
      care.
    • Support selection of the most relevant package according to the patient’s healthcare requirement.
    • Help prevent fraud and abuse.
The Standard Treatment Guidelines, STG - IT user manual & Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs) have also
been developed for ease of understanding of the States/UTs and EHCPs and are available at AB PM-JAY official
website.
